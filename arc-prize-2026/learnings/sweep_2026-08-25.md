## 1c. RESEARCH SWEEP

### PRIORITY ITEM — AVO / VISTA supervisor specification: **SOUGHT, NOT FOUND**

This was the single highest-value item on the brief: extract AVO's supervisor trigger conditions
and redirect content verbatim, so our **S1** arm could adopt a specification instead of inventing
one. **The specification is not disclosed anywhere public.** Checked the NVIDIA technical blog and
the arXiv paper (abstract + full HTML), **arXiv 2603.24517**, *"AVO: Agentic Variation Operators
for Autonomous Evolutionary Search"*.

What is actually published, verbatim [V]:

```
"The supervisor monitors the broader trajectory for stagnation or repeated
 unproductive cycles and can redirect the main agent toward alternative
 strategies when needed."

"To mitigate both, AVO incorporates a self-supervision mechanism that detects
 these scenarios and intervenes."

"Once triggered, the mechanism reviews the overall evolutionary trajectory and
 steers the search toward several candidate optimization directions."
```

- **Trigger conditions: NOT DISCLOSED.** No thresholds, no window sizes, no counters. [V]
- **Redirect content: NOT DISCLOSED.** No prompt text, no injected content. [V]
- **Ablation isolating the supervisor: DOES NOT EXIST.** The paper explicitly concedes
  *"this experiment does not isolate its individual contribution."* [V]
- Persistent memory is *"conversation history, which accumulates the full context of prior edits,
  compiler outputs, profiling results, and reasoning"* — i.e. functional description only. [V]

**Verdict: ADAPT, and DOWNGRADE S1.** The rationale that put S1 on the board was explicitly
*"adopting a specified mechanism beat inventing one when we went 1.33→1.59."* **There is no
specification to adopt.** Building S1 now means inventing a mechanism and attributing it to AVO —
exactly the move that rationale was meant to avoid.

### The finding that actually reweights the program: **VISTA reaches 100% with NO supervisor**

`https://vista-research.github.io/` — VISTA completes **all 183 levels across all 25 games, mean
score 100.00, in 7,542 actions** with Claude Opus 5.0 (GPT-5.6 Sol: 98.27, 22/25 perfect). Its
published architecture is observation → reasoning → action, plus memory access to earlier frames
via `inspect` / `read_pixels`, plus this [V]:

```
"When the model approaches its context limit, it writes a concise continuation
 state, then resumes from the current visual state in a fresh context."
```

**There is no stagnation detector, no replanning trigger, and no supervisor in it at all.** [V]

**Why this matters, stated carefully.** Our 08-22 read called the missing supervisor *"the largest
single gap."* That claim now has a clean counterexample: the *other* system that solves the same
183 levels does it **without one**. This does not prove a supervisor is worthless — AVO has one and
also hits 100% — but it does refute "necessary", and with **no ablation on either side** the
supervisor's contribution is **unmeasured in the literature, not merely unmeasured by us**.

What both 100% systems *do* share is **persistent memory across the trajectory** — which is
precisely our measured defect (i): the duck harness writes its carried world model **only** from
the model's VISIBLE message, and a reasoning model under a tool-calling grammar sends **97.64%** of
its content to the HIDDEN channel. VISTA's continuation-state-at-context-limit is a close cousin of
**P1** (persistent namespace) and the memory leg of **P2**.

**⇒ The evidence favours the MEMORY lane (P1/P2) over the SUPERVISOR lane (S1).** Recommend S1 drop
below P2 and be re-scoped as *"our own trigger from our own telemetry"* (45.2% immediate-repeat
rate; median 88% of wallclock after last level clear) rather than as an AVO port. Note the honest
counterweight: our harness-side stagnation guard `hard_noop_guard` **has never fired in 5,255
actions**, so a trigger that cannot fire remains the first risk to design against
(`feedback_guard_never_fired.md`).
