a green-looking read from a broken instrument. **Standing fix: every future 1c sweep must screen
the `/list/<cat>/recent` HTML, and use the API only to resolve IDs and abstracts.**

**(ii) My own listing-HTML parse produced wrong arXiv IDs, and I caught it only by cross-check.**
Cross-listed rows desynchronise the id-stream and the title-stream, so the naive parse mis-assigned
at least three IDs (it offered `2608.12959` and `2608.12962` for the *same* title, and returned a
theology paper and a wildfire paper for two "agent" titles). **Every ID quoted below was re-resolved
through the API `id_list`/`ti:` endpoint before being written down.** I also re-checked yesterday's
`ERSkill 2608.12720` against my parse's disagreeing `2608.12750`: **yesterday's sweep is correct**,
my parse was wrong. No correction is owed to the 08-15 record.

**(iii) A narrow, provable exception to the standing `LastSubmissionDate` rule.**
The sealed ★★ rule is right: `LastSubmissionDate` is a team's **latest** submission while `Score`
is their **best**, so in general the instrument cannot date a scoring run. **But when
ΔSubmissionCount == 1 between two snapshots and Score improved, the single new submission
*must* be the new best** — there is no other candidate. That condition holds for three teams this
window (§1.3), which is what makes those readings legitimate rather than a rule violation.

### 2.2 ★★ ADAPT #1 — arXiv:2608.12959, and why it is the paper we have been waiting for

> *"Latent world models are judged by how well they predict, so when planning fails at long horizons
> the natural reading is that the predictor degrades. … **the binding constraint is the planner's
> objective instead.** The predictor is not the limit: its imagined state seventy-five environment
> steps ahead is still only **0.189** as wrong as assuming the world froze, while the planner never
> imagines beyond twenty-five. … **The information is present throughout: a ridge probe recovers
> position from the frozen embedding at R² 0.9922.** … **Replacing only the objective, with nothing
> retrained and no GPU**, lifts goals reached at offset 100 from **26.0% to 98.0%**."*

**Why this is the right shape.** Our open question — *"forgetting REFUTED or
DELIVERY-WITHOUT-USE?"* — has been stuck because the two agents' positions (mech-C delivers on
**96.3%** of turns; behaviour does not move) looked irreconcilable except by hand-waving. This paper
is a fully worked instance of the reconciliation: **the information is provably present
(R² 0.9922), provably not the bottleneck, and the failure is entirely in the consumer's objective**
— a cost that tracks the thing you care about at only **r = 0.426**, saturates, and then
**decreases**, so that moving *away* from the goal can *lower* the cost. And crucially, the authors
show it is not one bad reimplementation: it reproduces in the original authors' released weights,
and across four checkpoints long-horizon success **rank-orders inversely with prediction accuracy**.

**What we would change, and how it would be scored.** Nothing yet — that is why this is ADAPT and
not ADOPT. What it buys is **the falsifier the panel has been missing**, and it is CPU-only on data
already on disk:

> **Do not ask "did the transition history arrive?" (answered: 96.3%). Ask "is the agent's
> action-selection criterion monotone in the thing that scores?"** Concretely: over archived P1 /
> mech-C logs, bin turns by realized level-progress and regress the agent's revealed preference
> (which action it took among those it enumerated) against it. **If the correlation is weak, or
> non-monotone past some horizon, the 96.3% null is explained and the fix is on the consumer side
> — where this paper says it can cost zero GPU.** If the correlation is strong, delivery-without-use
> is refuted for us and the forgetting camp keeps the field.

**Limits, stated plainly.** Different substrate: they have an *explicit* CEM objective they can
swap; we have an LLM policy with no externalised cost function, so the "replace only the objective"
move does not port literally. **The transferable content is the diagnostic, not the fix.** Any fix
that did follow would still have to clear **1.0826** on a pre-registered mean-of-4, and per ADAPT #3
must be scored **out-of-sample**, never on the logs used to find it.

### 2.3 ★ The elevated item — 2608.12321, and the uncomfortable prediction it makes

Not new (29 May 2026), zero prior hits in our record, and it names our question in its title:

> *"the constraint is internally encoded (**Knowledge**) … yet only sometimes routed into the
> decision (**Routing**) … probes on two open weights decode the constraint above **88%**, yet
> activation patching repairs one (**+6.4 nats**) and not the other (**−0.07**). On a mitigation
> frontier, **no prompted intervention reaches the repair corner: all inflate conservative bias
> through a single mediation pathway — prerequisite mention.** **Hidden-constraint failure is a
> routing problem, not a knowledge problem.**"*

Read against our own record this is close to a pre-registration of the result we got:

| Their finding | Our measurement |
|---|---|
| The constraint is encoded at **>88%** probe accuracy | mech-C **delivered on 96.3%** of turns; EFFNOTE **93.8%** |
| …and behaviour still does not follow | …and behaviour **did not move** on either |
| **No prompted intervention reaches the repair corner** | **Every intervention we have run is prompt-side** |
| Prompted fixes instead **inflate conservative defaulting** | SteerBench-Work (§2.1 #9) independently measures **28.1% wrongly-hold vs 1.0% wrongly-allow** |
| Repair works on one model and not another (+6.4 vs −0.07 nats) | We have **one** engine and have never tested a second |

**Three consequences the panel should carry, none of which is an ADOPT.**

1. **The 96.3% null is the literature's expected outcome, not an anomaly.** "Delivery-without-use"
   stops being a hypothesis competing with "forgetting" and becomes the *default* reading; the
   burden shifts to whoever wants to claim forgetting.
2. **It predicts that raising the 31,744-token ceiling will null too.** Open question #2 from the
   08-15 brief now has a stated prior: more delivered context, same routing, same behaviour.
   **That makes it a cheap, high-information experiment — pre-register the null.** (The ceiling is
   still ours: both checkpoints declare `max_position_embeddings: 262144`, and today's FP8 repack
   card independently restates **"Context Length: 262,144 natively"**.)
3. **It is the strongest evidence yet that the engine matters** — repair succeeded on one model and
   failed on another with the same probe accuracy — which is a *routing* argument for the Qwen3.8
   swap, entirely independent of the forum's vibes and of the vendor's benchmarks. **It is also the
   only argument in this sweep that connects the two sections.** It does not license a submission;
   it does raise the value of the screen already authorised on 08-15.

**Not adopted because:** it is a May paper (no novelty claim), its instrument is **activation
patching on open weights**, and the Kaggle serving rail gives us logits, not a residual stream.
There is no version of this we can run this week.
