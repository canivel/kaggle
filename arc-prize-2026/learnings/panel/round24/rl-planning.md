# R24 review — reviewer #1, RL & Planning (search/exploration + planning falsifiability)

## Summary

The lane ranking ((a) > (c) > (b)) is defensible and the "spend zero pushes on two free offline falsifiers first" sequencing is the right instinct, but the proposal's three authorised falsifiers are each mis-specified in a way that lets them pass without testing the claim they are attached to, and §2.3 — the "metric fact that changed today" that motivates both the co-primary metric and P1's attractiveness — is contradicted by the team's own `runs/kernel_pulls/war_eval_v1/benchmark.json`, in which all 25 games terminated on **wall clock (~7,920 s each, ~54 s per scored action)** and never approached an action cap. As authorised, P1 relocates state without any pre-registered measurement of whether exploration behaviour changes; the fixes are cheap and specific, so this is a revision, not a rejection.

## Objections

**[FATAL] §2.3's budget model is wrong on the team's own data, and it is load-bearing for the one authorised push.** §2.3 asserts the Kaggle metric's action-efficiency structure makes in-sandbox reasoning "cost zero scored actions" and a persistent namespace "doubly attractive," and on that basis promotes `actions_per_level_completed` to co-primary. I read `runs/kernel_pulls/war_eval_v1/benchmark.json`: `final_wallclock_seconds` is **7920.2–7939.9 s for every one of the 25 games**, identical to three decimals — the run is uniformly **time-terminated**, not action-terminated. 3,638 scored actions over ~7,920 s/game is **~54 s per scored action**, and per-action `wallclock_seconds` in `history` runs 125→173 s early in a game; the 5× caps implied by `base_actions_per_level` are nowhere near binding (sk48 used 266 actions with a level-1 cap alone of 305 and finished 0 levels). The binding resource is LLM latency under 16-way concurrency, not the scored-action budget. Therefore in-sandbox reasoning is **not** free: every extra token and every sandbox round-trip converts directly into fewer environment actions inside a fixed wall clock, which under "zero credit for unfinished levels" is the most direct route to *losing* completions. This is the same latency mechanism that produced R23's NC-14 mechanism-null. Required fix: re-derive §2.3 against the wall-clock-bound regime, state explicitly that P1's cost is paid in seconds rather than actions, and either drop `actions_per_level_completed` as co-primary or report it jointly with `actions_per_game_hour` — as written the ratio improves whenever an arm merely thinks longer per turn.

**[MAJOR] §6.1's arm-defining invariant guards the wrong resource.** The invariant requires `evicted_chars` and the `_trim_messages_for_context()` output to be byte-identical to baseline — that correctly forecloses a *context* confound, which is what A22 bought. But given the objection above, P1's plausible effect path is **resource**, not context: 16 long-lived sandbox children, per-game `RLIMIT_CPU` re-accounting (`python_tool_sandbox.py` currently sets `RLIMIT_CPU = timeout_seconds + 1` **per call**, ~L252-258), and extra tokens per turn all change per-action latency on a shared GPU, and per-action latency is what determines how many actions a game gets. A screen that passes non-harm could be passing because it took fewer, better actions, or failing because it took fewer actions full stop, and §6.1 cannot distinguish them. Add to §6.1: pre-registered parity reporting of **mean and p95 seconds-per-scored-action, total actions per game, generated tokens per turn, and per-game peak child count/RSS**, with a pre-registered voiding fraction if seconds-per-action moves beyond a stated band. This is free — all four are already in `history` and the safety-canary plumbing.

**[MAJOR] `namespace_reuse_rate` is a syntactic name-count, not an exploration measurement, and 0.15 is unanchored.** K4 is the proposal's headline "falsifier that does not need the score," and I accept its logic — but as defined ("fraction of turns that reference a name defined in a previous turn") it is satisfied by a model that defines one helper function and calls it again, with the policy otherwise unchanged. There is also no control: under the ephemeral baseline the rate is structurally 0 (NameError), so 0.15 is compared against nothing, and §6.4's boast that "nothing was retuned" does not cover it — unlike the −0.128/−1.0 harm gates, 0.15 is **new and inherited from no prereg**. Three fixes, all cheap: (i) run the §5.4 transcript-forensics/affordance-compliance pass **before** sealing, and derive the floor from the observed distribution of cross-turn name references in existing `python`-tool calls rather than picking a constant; (ii) require the reused name to be *load-bearing* — defined ≥2 turns earlier and referenced in a call that emits an action — and report that as a separate rate; (iii) report the full distribution across the 25 games, not a pooled scalar, since a single verbose game can carry the pooled rate over 0.15 on its own.

**[MAJOR] Nothing in the authorised experiment measures whether exploration behaviour changes; S3's decision rule promotes on absence of harm.** This is my core remit finding. In planning terms, a persistent namespace supplies memory; it does not supply a policy-improvement operator. Model-maintenance, planning against the model, and an action-selection criterion (novelty / information gain / goal-directed search) are the things that change exploration, and the proposal defers all of them to L4 behind S5 and a governance ruling. The P1 screen's endpoint set is {non-harm Δlc, reported actions/lc, name-reuse ≥ 0.15}, so its only two outcomes are NULL or "proceed" — it has **zero power to detect benefit**, yet S3 reads "P1 PASS ⇒ S4." Fix: pre-register at least one behavioural exploration statistic with a direction, computed for free from the existing action history — **redundant-action rate** (fraction of actions repeating an (observed-frame-hash, action) pair already tried in the current level), **distinct frames visited per level**, **ACTION6 coordinate dispersion / distinct cells probed** over the 4,096-cell space, and **actions-to-first-level-clear**. If a persistent world-model namespace does not move *any* of those, the substrate demonstrably relocated state and changed nothing, and that should be a pre-registered null even when non-harm passes.

**[MAJOR] S1b tests the banking root cause on the games where the failure was never observed, and cannot test the blocker that actually produced the zero.** The proposal gates S1b on "step-0 `frame_divergence` must clear on the 11-game prefix-splice-safe set." Per `learnings/panel/r16_circulation.md` L1258, that 11-game set (ar25, bp35, ft09, lf52, lp85, ls20, r11l, sp80, su15, tn36, tu93) is *defined* as the clean, non-phase-aliased set where pruning was already legal; the observed step-0 divergences in `runs/war_eval_v1/bank_fire_validation.json` are on **sc25 and m0r0**, both of which are excluded from it as FULL-REPLAY-ONLY. The gate is therefore near-vacuous and its kill condition ("persisting on the safe set ⇒ root cause misidentified") fires only if something unrelated breaks. Worse: the same file's own verdict records `"time_starvation_reproduces_eval_zero": true`, and `bank_fire_validation.py`'s docstring states the war-eval produced zero replay events because "every run ended at its time budget, so `_bank`'s soft-time check skipped" (`bank_min_time_s = 120`) — which my benchmark read confirms for all 25 games. §3(c) names **one** blocker (N5 `prune_trace` phase desync) when the record contains **two**, and the offline re-fire runs with unlimited soft time by construction, so it cannot touch the one that actually zeroed the lane. Fix: re-point the S1b gate at **sc25 and m0r0** (pass = step-0 divergence clears there under full unpruned replay from RESET), state the time-starvation blocker explicitly in §3(c), and add a pre-registered in-kernel feasibility condition (what fraction of games end with `soft_time_remaining ≥ 120 s`) before any banking push is contemplated.

**[MAJOR] S1's gate is a fidelity metric that §6.4 simultaneously declares non-load-bearing, and it has no number.** S1's proceed-gate is "carrier set must **expand beyond ~4 games**" measured by accepted transition match + coverage. Two problems. First, §6.4's Goodhart row commits — correctly, citing Tycho H2 (88.1% accepted match, still lost) and our own sp80/lp85/sb26 collapse — that fidelity is "a **mechanism canary only**" and "cannot gate." S1 gates on it. That is a direct internal contradiction, and it matters because `exec_wm/scale_summary.md` shows exactly the pathology: seven games at 100.00% held-out `state_exact` whose on-trajectory behaviour the proposal itself reports collapsing to 0.026–0.879. Second, "~4" is a tilde, not a threshold, and the r16 consumer ruling names **three** ship-now carriers (ft09/tn36/tu93) — so the baseline is ambiguous between 3 and 4 and a one-game move could be scored either way. Fix: pre-register (i) the exact current carrier list, (ii) a per-game admission criterion as a numeric pair (accepted match ≥ X% **at a stated rollout horizon H** with coverage ≥ Y%), (iii) H itself — compounding error under state threading is the whole question — and (iv) a multiplicity note, since 24 games screened against a threshold will produce crossings by chance. And either drop the fidelity gate in favour of L0's carrier expansion being *reported*, or withdraw §6.4's "fidelity cannot gate" commitment; you cannot hold both.

**[MINOR] The differential hypothesis against the duck's existing cross-turn channel is never stated.** `tool_agent.py` already carries `_summarized_knowledge` — `world_model`, `goal_model`, `action_model`, `recent_findings`, `open_questions`, `current_plan`, `cross_level_notes` — into every prompt (~L1311). The duck therefore already has a persistent, cross-turn world-model-and-plan slot; P1 changes its **medium** (executable/typed/unbounded vs textual/summarised), not its existence. §3(a)'s "missing: a persistent namespace" overstates the delta. State the differential hypothesis and measure it for free: content overlap between what lands in the namespace and what lands in `_summarized_knowledge`. If they carry the same content, P1 is a medium change with a latency cost — which, given the FATAL above, is the pessimistic default.

**[MINOR] Addendum contradictions the proposal must absorb.** (i) §6.5's arithmetic is one draw stale (n=25/0.9384/0.1569 → n=26/0.9365/0.1540, threshold 1.0823 → 1.0773); harmless since no draw is requested, but fix at seal. (ii) `_ADDENDUM2` §B2 withdraws §3(c)'s "field is near-empty across two consecutive sweeps" premise — the proposal must restate lane (c)'s down-ranking as resting on the generalisation rail, in those words, and should note 2608.05810 (VaG: skill accumulation non-monotonic past a pool-size threshold) as an additional caution. (iii) `_ADDENDUM` §A3's Feng report is design evidence with zero efficacy evidence from a 177th-place entrant; its only legitimate use is retiring "implementable at 27B?" for P3, and the two-timescale refinement should be logged as an option, not adopted. (iv) On §A5: a reviewer-model change is a real continuity break for score *trends*, and I endorse the instruction that no minute rest on a round-over-round comparison — but it does not compromise this review's objections, which are anchored in repo artifacts a reader can re-check.

## Questions for the authors

1. Given that all 25 war-eval games terminated at ~7,920 s wall clock with ~54 s per scored action, what is the projected **latency cost per turn** of a persistent sandbox child under 16-way concurrency, and what is the pre-registered band beyond which the P1 arm is void for resource non-parity?
2. What is the theory of change from "variables persist across turns" to "more levels completed"? Name the mechanism in one sentence and the statistic that would show it firing.
3. Under the per-call `RLIMIT_CPU = timeout_seconds + 1`, what per-game CPU limit will the long-lived child get, and does that limit differ from the cumulative CPU the baseline's ephemeral children consume? If it is larger, the arm has more compute than the control — how is that not an M3-class confound?
4. Was step-0 `frame_divergence` ever observed on any of the 11 prefix-splice-safe games? If not, what does the S1b gate as written falsify?
5. What fraction of games in war_eval_v1 ended with `soft_time_remaining_seconds() ≥ 120`? If the answer is near zero, what does an S1b PASS entitle lane (c) to?
6. §6.4 says fidelity "cannot gate"; §4 S1 gates on fidelity. Which commitment survives, and if S1's gate survives, what is the numeric admission criterion and the rollout horizon?
7. What is the baseline `namespace_reuse_rate`, and if it is 0 by construction, on what evidence is 0.15 the right floor rather than 0.05 or 0.50?
8. If P1 passes non-harm and reuse ≥ 0.15 but every exploration statistic (redundant-action rate, distinct frames, ACTION6 dispersion) is unchanged, does the lane proceed to S4? A "yes" makes K4 the only real falsifier in the design.

I take the A22 death record, the −0.403 eviction correlation, the M3 confound analysis, and the χ²/change-point stationarity work as given and do not re-litigate them. I cannot assess licence status of the Schema HF trace set, the Tycho and Prime Agent source claims (I did not fetch either repo), the arXiv items in either addendum, or the exact form of the Kaggle scoring function beyond what `docs/community_research_apr1.md` is cited as saying. I have no view on whether workstation LLM authoring is in-bounds under the zero-budget rail (§5.3.1) — that is a governance question, not a planning one, though I note its resolution gates L1 and therefore the entire artifact half.

## Verdict: MAJOR-REVISION

The lane selection is right and the cost discipline is real; I would have voted ACCEPT on the decision alone. I cannot, because all three authorised falsifiers can pass without testing their claims (S1 gates on a metric the document says cannot gate and does so without a number; S1b is pointed at the games that never failed and is blind to the blocker that actually zeroed the lane; K4 counts names rather than behaviour), and because §2.3 — which supplies the rationale for the one push and for the new co-primary metric — is contradicted by the team's own `war_eval_v1` benchmark. Every fix I have named is cheap, mostly free, and mostly recovers information already on disk; the revision should be one pass, not a new round of argument.
