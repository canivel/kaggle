## Summary (2 sentences)
The brief correctly fires its pre-registered watch-rule and refuses to fire gated draws on autopilot, which is the right instinct; but the quantitative guard it builds is internally inconsistent — the claimed bias direction under a step-down is wrong, the "corrected" bar 1.1701 silently drops the control-uncertainty term its own sealed bar 1.0970 includes, and the headline probabilities (0.38% / 18.5%) do not reproduce from any stated model. The change-point p=0.0032 is also reported as if it were unconditional when the test was only run because the data triggered it, so the central R23 decision is currently being framed by statistics that cannot be taken at face value.

## Objections

[MAJOR] Bias-direction claim is wrong for the step-down branch — The brief asserts "if the process stepped down or is under-dispersed vs seal, a sealed-control test is biased TOWARD spurious promotion." That is only true for the dispersion branch (true σ > sealed s = 0.1343). If the level stepped down (0.973→0.665, the very change-point the scan found), gated draws under the null of no-treatment-effect are drawn from the *lower* regime, making P(mean of 4 > 1.0970) collapse — bias toward spurious *non*-promotion. Conflating these two opposite-signed failure modes into one "dangerous direction" misstates the risk calculus for choosing among options (a)/(b)/(c); rewrite §1 with the two scenarios separated and their respective α and power computed.

[MAJOR] The "corrected" bar 1.1701 is inconsistent with the sealed bar's own construction and is anti-conservative under the proposal's premise — Reverse-engineering: 1.0970 = 0.9727 + 1.645·0.1343·√(1/4 + 1/15), i.e., a two-sample bar that includes control-mean uncertainty. But 1.1701 = 0.9727 + 1.645·0.24·√(1/4) — the √(1/15) term vanished. Applying the sealed bar's own formula with σ=0.24 gives ≈ **1.195**. If you adopt guard (a), you are adopting a bar that is loose by ~0.025 under your own model; either justify dropping the control-uncertainty term (you can't — the control mean is an estimate either way) or publish 1.195.

[MAJOR] The 0.38% and 18.5% consecutive-pair probabilities are unreproducible from stated inputs — Under N(0.9727, 0.1343): P(draw ≤ 0.80)² ≈ 0.99%, P(≤0.68)·P(≤0.65) ≈ 0.012%; neither is 0.38%. Under σ=0.24 with either stated mean, P(pair ≤ 0.80)² lands at 5.6–7.8%, not 18.5%. These two numbers are the entire quantitative basis for "not tail-consistent under seal, ordinary under σ=0.24," which in turn motivates the whole guard. Publish the exact event definition (thresholds, joint vs. scanned-over-19-draws, Gaussian vs. t, assumed mean under the yw8837 regime) and the reproduction script, as was done for the ledger stats.

[MAJOR] Conditional inference: the change-point p=0.0032 is computed as if the test were unconditional, but it was run only because the watch-rule fired — Running a max-Welch scan *after* observing two consecutive lows, with the split landing exactly at the trigger, inflates apparent significance ("test the data that made you look"). Compounding this, an n₂=2 endpoint segment whose two values happen to be near-equal (s₂≈0.021) mechanically produces |t|=8.64. The concrete fix: simulate ≥10k stationary N(0.9727, 0.1343) ledgers of n=19, apply the *same* pipeline (watch-rule trigger → max-Welch scan), and report the conditional false-alarm rate; the qualitative hand-wave "two-point breaks are what these tests over-call" should be that number. Also state which CUSUM h was pre-registered — reporting "crosses h=4 not h=5" without a pre-committed h is threshold-shopping.

[MAJOR] σ≈0.24 is borrowed from an external team with no matching evidence — The corrected bar and the "ordinary under σ=0.24" claim both load-bear on yw8837's published duck-family dispersion, yet our own n=19 record gives s=0.159 and the sealed control s=0.134. No evidence is presented that yw8837's fork, hardware, or eval cadence matches ours (their σ could reflect a heterogeneous mixture of variants). Before sealing bar (a), either show yw8837's setup is exchangeable with ours (fork diff + submission cadence) or estimate σ from our own contemporaneous data — which is exactly what option (b)/(c) would produce, and is another reason to prefer them.

[MAJOR] No power analysis accompanies any of the three guard options — Raising the bar to 1.17 (or 1.195) with n=4 gated draws and σ=0.24 gives roughly 30–40% power against a true lift of +0.15 (the seed-level Δlc scale observed), i.e., the A/B as guarded will likely return an uninformative negative. Option (b)'s paired design with 4 pairs has ~3 df and is similarly weak unless the pairing correlation is high. R23 cannot rationally choose among (a)/(b)/(c) without a power table (n, σ, bar) for each; supply it before ratification.

[MINOR] "Both seeds PASS with positive direction" over-reads noise — Δlc = +0.152 with sd 0.537 over 16 paired items is t≈1.13, p≈0.27; +0.112 similarly. As a *non-harm* screen this is fine, but the "positive direction" framing invites the panel to bank an effect that is fully consistent with zero; label these explicitly as non-significant.

[MINOR] Missing serial-dependence check — Two adjacent lows are as consistent with positive autocorrelation (e.g., a slow platform-side covariate) as with a level shift, and autocorrelation invalidates both the pair-probability arithmetic and the independence assumptions in the A/B. Report lag-1 autocorrelation and a runs test on the n=19 ledger in the stationarity memo.
