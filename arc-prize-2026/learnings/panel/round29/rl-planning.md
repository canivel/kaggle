## Summary (2 sentences)

The brief's evidence hygiene (tagging, control arms, ledger discipline) is well above field standard, and the identification of `bench` as the campaign's first placebo-controlled comparator is the right instinct. But the load-bearing decision — "run the 2v2 bench and let it kill or spare the TV28 arm" — is statistically unsound as specified: a 2-per-arm comparison on one game cannot support even a kill decision at any defensible error rate, the kill gate is not numerically pre-registered, and the document contains no strategy whatsoever for closing a gap it correctly measures at +4.27σ against its own best config.

## Objections

**[FATAL] The 2v2 bench cannot license a kill decision, and the proposal's own variance data proves it.** The minimum achievable p-value for a permutation test with n=2 per arm is 1/C(4,2) = 1/6 ≈ 0.167 (one-sided) — even the maximally separated outcome {A both low, B both high} occurs by chance under exchangeability one time in six. Against a within-config spread whose own documented realization is 1.82-and-0.00 on byte-identical code, ANY 2v2 gap is well inside noise. The proposal hedges ("kill instrument… only on a large gap") but never states the gap, the statistic, or the error rate it accepts, which violates the same falsifiable-eval discipline (pre-registered numeric gates, hash-locked before the run) the campaign claims to operate under. Fix: pre-register a numeric kill gate now, and accumulate replicates across multiple bench sessions (n≥6/arm is reachable in 3 nights at zero draws) before any stop/continue decision is read out. As written, action #1 will produce a number that will be over-read regardless of the "do not let it promote anything" incantation — there is no symmetric "do not let it kill anything at n=2" guard, and killing is exactly what the handoff invites.

**[MAJOR] Treatment-can-fire is unverified for the bench itself, so the A/B may be stock-vs-stock.** The Gaps section concedes `install()` is blanket-guarded and falls back to stock on any error, and that the 1.62 run's graft banner was never pulled — so "13 grafts were live" is [UNK] on the run. The identical failure mode applies to the bench: if `install()` silently falls back in arm A, the instrument compares placebo to placebo and returns a clean null that will be read as "the stack does nothing." Action #1 verifies the `TRUE_SUBMISSION` guard but does not require asserting the A-arm `TAAF_GRAFTS FEATURES=...` banner in the bench artifacts before the readout is admitted. Add that assertion as a validity gate: no banner in A → run is VOID, not a null.

**[MAJOR] Within-session replicas are not independent, and the design has an unaddressed order/budget confound.** Four replicas at the documented 7920s per-game wall-clock is ~8.8h — essentially the entire session — so late replicas plausibly run under truncated budget, and the proposal's own find #7 says the wall-clock/token budget is the binding constraint on every archived game. If the tag order A0/B0/A1/B1 executes sequentially, arm is confounded with session position. Additionally, under a binding token budget, suppressing graft text in arm B frees tokens — the comparison is "graft info + budget cost" vs "no info + budget refund," which is a legitimate treatment definition but must be stated, because a null could mean "the information is worth exactly its token cost." Fix: verify execution order in the artifact paths, counterbalance or randomize if possible, and log per-replica termination cause (wall-clock vs completion).

**[MAJOR] One game (m0r0) cannot kill a stack scored over multiple games; the extrapolation rule is unstated.** Graft effects are almost certainly heterogeneous across games (find #7's own data shows game-specific cap saturation). A large A<B gap on m0r0 licenses at most "stop on m0r0-like games," yet the handoff frames the readout as a decision about the whole TV28 arm. State before the run: what result on m0r0 generalizes to what claim about the config, and why was m0r0 chosen (by Tennant, not by us — his selection criteria are unknown and may favor games where his grafts look best or worst).

**[MAJOR] Config mean is the selection statistic, but the score distribution is plausibly zero-inflated, and the proposal never checks.** Byte-identical 1.82/0.00, plus our own 0.41 outlier, suggests a mixture: a competence distribution plus a failure mode (crash/timeout → ~0). If so, config mean conflates capability with infrastructure reliability, and the right ledger statistics are failure rate and conditional mean separately — a config with a fixable 15% crash rate is a different object from a config with a low ceiling. Before re-deriving the ledger (action #3), classify the near-zero draws by cause from run logs. This also bears on `project_arc_final_selection_rule` itself: if the rule is mean-based and the distribution is bimodal, the rule is selecting on the wrong functional and should be revisited — which I cannot do from here because the rule's semantics aren't in the document (see Q1).

**[MAJOR] The proposal has no exploration policy, only measurement policy — and its own numbers say all current arms are dead.** Field floor mean 1.5413 sits +4.27σ below the top-10 line, which moved +0.18 in a night; the gap is widening. Every action in the handoff is defensive (measure, pin, correct ledger, don't spend). That is locally correct per-decision and globally guarantees the objective is never reached: this is a bandit where every known arm's mean is far below target and the plan allocates zero effort to arm generation. Even if the honest answer is "no candidate mechanism exists this week," the strategy document must say what would generate one (the never-built stagnation supervisor? a fork of a *scoring* lineage rather than Tennant's non-scoring one?) and what evidence would trigger spending on it. A plan that can only lose more slowly is a plan to lose.

**[MINOR] Inconsistent inference standard on single draws.** Our +2.01σ draw is (correctly) labeled variance; Youssef Nader's +1.58 and Liao Zixu's +0.92 single draws are labeled "capability steps." Without knowing their config variances — and the field-wide evidence says variance of the 1.82/0.00 class exists — a single large draw from a 12-sub team is not distinguishable from one tail draw of a high-variance config. The "ladder hypothesis, ninth day" language is accreting confidence from observations the document's own evidence-discipline section says cannot carry it. Downgrade to UNKNOWN consistently or state the variance model under which their draws are steps and ours was not.

**[MINOR] "Zero draws" is not zero cost.** One bench run is ~9h of GPU session — a large fraction of a weekly Kaggle GPU quota, and the objection compounds if my n≥6/arm fix is adopted (3 sessions). Budget it explicitly against whatever else needs GPU (any future arm-generation work), rather than pricing it as free.
