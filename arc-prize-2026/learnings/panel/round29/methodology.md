## Summary (2 sentences)

This brief has unusually good statistical hygiene at the descriptive level (leave-one-out σ on the 2.05, max-vs-mean discipline, base-rate framing of Δ/draw, refusal to conclude from the n=1 fork draw), and its central recommendation — run a zero-draw paired A/B before spending another draw — is the right instinct. But the plan's load-bearing inferential machinery is defective in three places: the bench "kill instrument" has no written, numeric decision rule and no noise estimate on its own outcome variable; the control-arm and author-comparison inferences are invalidated by max-statistic mechanics; and the config-mean statistic that the entire endgame selection rule depends on has no demonstrated pre-registered membership rule.

## Objections

**[MAJOR] The bench pre-registration is asserted, not written — a "kill on a large gap" rule with "large" undefined is not a pre-registration.** The handoff says "Pre-register the read before the data lands" but the document contains no outcome variable (score per replica? levels cleared? actions?), no numeric kill threshold, and no noise scale for the bench metric — the 1.82/0.00 variance figure is *LB commit-run* variance, not within-run replicate variance on m0r0, which is currently unmeasured. Without these, the analyst reads the 2v2 gap after seeing it and decides post hoc whether it is "large," which is exactly the retune-and-rerun failure the project's own v0.9 discipline prohibits. Fix before execution: commit (hash-locked, per your own ISA.lock pattern) the outcome variable, the kill rule expressed against the pooled within-arm spread (e.g., a 2-df t on A0/A1 vs B0/B1 — note critical t₀.₉₅,₂ ≈ 4.3, so state honestly how enormous the gap must be), and the explicit decision map {kill → stop TV28; anything else → no update}.

**[MAJOR] The instrument has never passed a falsification self-test — run an A/A before trusting an A/B.** Your own house rule: a harness that cannot demonstrate its error behavior on a known null is inadmissible. One additional free GPU session with all four replicas on the same arm (A0/A1/A2/A3) would (a) verify the tag-from-artifact-path labeling end to end, (b) give the first-ever estimate of within-run replicate variance on the bench outcome, and (c) reveal whether the instrument produces spurious "large gaps" under the null. Given that runs cost zero draws, skipping this is unjustifiable; the kill threshold in Objection 1 cannot be set sensibly without it.

**[MAJOR] Possible position/budget confound in the 4-replica design.** Cell-7 telemetry (find #7) says every archived game ends on the 7920 s per-game wall clock; 4 × 7920 s = 8.8 h against the ~9 h session you budget, so the final replica (B1, by tag order) is at risk of truncation. Because arms are interleaved the first-order confound is mitigated, but a truncated B1 systematically shortchanges the control arm, biasing the comparison toward "A not worse" — i.e., toward *never firing* the only conclusion the instrument is allowed to produce. Verify per-replica time accounting in bench's own artifacts, or pre-commit to analyzing A0/B0 only if B1 is truncated.

**[MAJOR] The control-arm inference ("commodity-engine story is weak") is close to zero-power by construction.** Score is a max over submissions; under a stationary draw distribution, the probability that a team's (n+1)th draw sets a new personal record is ≈ 1/(n+1), so Tufa (123rd draw) and MindsAI (133rd draw) each had a ~0.8% chance of moving *even if nothing about the regime changed*. Two flat observations with expected movement ≈ 0.02 events cannot distinguish "shared regime" from its negation; the verbatim `lb_diff` conclusion should be struck or re-derived with record-value mechanics, because it is feeding the strategic picture. The same mechanics partially explain the "80% gained 0.00" base rate — it conflates teams at wildly different n.

**[MAJOR] The tennant comparison (his 1.93/39 vs our 2.05/126) is a max-vs-max fallacy the document itself warns against, deployed twice as evidence.** Expected maximum grows with draw count; under *identical* underlying config distributions, the team with 126 draws is expected to sit above the team with 39. "His board row is below ours" therefore carries almost no information about the grafts' effect and should not appear as a load-bearing clause in Handoff 2. The decision not to redraw survives on its other legs (n=1, author's own disclosure) — remove the invalid leg so the ledger doesn't inherit it.

**[MAJOR] The config mean 1.5413 — the statistic the final selection rule runs on — has no demonstrated membership rule or version identity.** The eight-draw floor config excludes the 1.15 and 0.41 draws visible in the own-row sequence; nothing in the document states the a priori rule that excludes them, and post-hoc exclusion of low draws inflates the config mean that `project_arc_final_selection_rule` selects on. Symmetrically: you correctly demand version-pinning for the TV28 fork, but never state whether the field-floor filler's own bundle is version-pinned — if not, the same "no config identity" objection destroys the n=8 mean too. State the membership rule, confirm it predates the excluded draws, and audit the floor config's pinning with the same rigor applied to the fork.

**[MINOR] The "ladder hypothesis confirmed for a ninth day" narration is unfalsifiable as operated.** With ~292 submitters per window drawing from a heavy-tailed Δ distribution, the daily appearance of one or two large single-draw order statistics (max 1.58 vs median 0.23) is near-guaranteed regardless of any shared mechanism; observing it "again" is not confirmation. The falsifiers listed (public artifacts appearing; sub-count spikes) are conditions that would *refute*, but no daily observation short of them can *disconfirm*, so the daily "confirmed" language overstates evidential accrual. Since the attached action is "watch," this is minor — but stop counting confirmation days.

**[MINOR] Stale promotion bar governs tonight's decisions.** The bar (1.089, from n=37 as of 08-20) is invoked by the standing rule in Handoff 2 while Handoff 3 admits ten draws are missing from the distribution it was computed on. Re-derive the ledger *before* applying the standing rule tonight, not as a parallel chore — ordering matters when the rule gates the head submission.
