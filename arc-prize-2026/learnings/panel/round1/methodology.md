## Summary (2 sentences)
The proposal's central methodological claim — "we can measure; they gamble" — rests on a local A/B instrument whose statistical power is never established: 3-seed sweeps against a baseline the Duck author himself describes as high-variance, with gating thresholds (+3 levels, 30% std reduction) that are undecidable at n=3. The strategic phasing and asset reuse are sensible, but the inference machinery that every merge/kill decision depends on is currently a placebo, and the plan additionally overfits to the 25 public games while the money is on 55 unseen ones.

## Objections

**[FATAL] The "≥0.21 public-LB noise" figure is derived from n=2 and then used as a load-bearing constant.** The only cited evidence is a single pair (v31 = 0.43 vs v32 = 0.22). A difference of two draws is one observation of the noise distribution; it gives you neither σ nor its shape, and the later claim "we know σ≈0.1+" is asserted, not measured. Yet this number justifies the entire local-first architecture and the Phase-4 max-of-N strategy. Fix: in Phase 0, submit the *identical* frozen Duck fork 4–6 times (the plan already budgets daily slots for fillers — spend them on this) and report the empirical σ with a confidence interval before treating the LB as unusable or as a lottery with known odds.

**[FATAL] The Phase-2 variance-reduction criterion ("std of RHAE across 3 seeds reduced ≥30%") is statistically undecidable as written.** A ratio of two variances each estimated from n=3 follows F(2,2), whose 95% interval spans roughly two orders of magnitude; observing a 30% sample-std drop is fully consistent with zero true change, and observing a 30% *increase* is consistent with a real improvement. Any merge/kill decision keyed to this criterion is a coin flip dressed as measurement. Fix: either drop variance as a gated criterion, or estimate it from ≥10 seeds (the budget permits this — see the power objection below), or gate on a paired per-game statistic where 25 games × 3 seeds gives ~75 paired observations instead of 3.

**[MAJOR] No power analysis anywhere, and the seed budget is arbitrary rather than derived.** Every phase gate ("+≥3 levels aggregate," "+≥4 levels," "+≥2 levels or ≥15% fewer actions") is a point threshold with no null distribution: what is the seed-to-seed spread of aggregate levels for the *unchanged* Phase-0 build? If identical builds differ by ±4 levels across seeds (plausible given Duck's documented irreproducibility of its own 1.21), then a +3-level gate at 3 seeds has both high false-pass and high false-kill rates — and the kill criteria ("fails in 2 consecutive ablations") will execute real components or ship noise. Fix: run the frozen Phase-0 build for 8–10 seeds once ($10–15 at the quoted $0.40/h — trivial), publish the resulting noise band, and set every subsequent gate as "delta exceeds the 90th percentile of the identical-build null," using paired per-game permutation tests rather than aggregate point thresholds.

**[MAJOR] Sequential selection with compounding winner's curse.** Each phase's baseline is the *selected best* build of the previous phase, so each measured delta is conditioned on having passed a noisy gate — a classic upward bias that accumulates. The Phase-4 target ("+9 local levels compounding") is a sum of three selection-biased estimates, and the "fix the two best configs" step from the full ablation matrix (≥8 configs × 3 seeds) adds a max-over-configs selection on top, with no correction and no fresh confirmation run. Fix: after freezing the two final configs on Sep 22, re-run both on *fresh seeds never used for selection* and report those numbers as the unbiased local estimate; expect and budget for shrinkage.

**[MAJOR] Adaptive overfitting to the 25 public games contradicts the generality thesis.** Every gate from July through September evaluates on the same 25 games, dozens of times, with components accepted precisely when they help those games (e.g., "BFS instantly closes the 14 games our solver already owns" — that is per-game fit by construction, even without game identifiers). The held-out self-testing appears only in the October sketch, *after* all design decisions are locked. Fix: split now — e.g., develop and gate on 18 games, hold out 7 touched only at phase boundaries — or generate procedural held-out games from day one; report the dev/holdout gap at each phase as the overfitting canary.

**[MAJOR] The Phase-4 "best Kaggle draw ≥1.6" success criterion measures luck, not the system, and max-of-N selection does not transfer to the private set.** The maximum of 6–8 draws from a noisy distribution is inflated by construction; declaring success on it is exactly the "hill-climbing on dice rolls" the proposal accuses the fork swarm of. Moreover, if the final ranking is on the private ~55 games, the public-LB max is selection on noise orthogonal to private performance (and every team can play the same lottery, so relative expected rank gain ≈ 0). Fix: state the success criterion as the *mean* of the Phase-4 draws with a CI, and specify the final-submission selection rule in advance (which config, chosen on what statistic).

**[MINOR] The 1.7–2.0 top-10 extrapolation has no stated model.** Six days of post-release diffusion extrapolated to three months, with no functional form, no error bar, and it silently sets the bar the whole plan is calibrated against. State the extrapolation method and a range, or label it a guess.

**[MINOR] "No game regresses by >1 level in ≥2/3 seeds" is an ill-posed multiplicity trap.** With 25 games × 3 seeds, some game will show a 1-level seed-level regression by chance under any true improvement; the criterion as worded will either never fire or fire spuriously depending on interpretation. Rewrite as a per-game paired test with an explicit false-discovery control (e.g., Benjamini–Hochberg at q=0.1 across the 25 games).

## Questions for the authors
1. What is the seed-to-seed standard deviation of aggregate levels (and of RHAE) for a *frozen, unchanged* build on your local harness? If unknown, why were 3 seeds chosen before knowing it?
2. The A40 budget is 40 h/wk but a 3-seed sweep is 8 GPU-h; you can afford 10+ seeds per decision point for under $20. What justifies not doing so at merge gates?
3. How exactly will you correct the final local performance estimate for having been selected through ~5 sequential noisy gates plus an 8-config ablation max?
4. What is the pre-registered decision rule for the *final* Nov 2 submission — which statistic, computed on which data, selects between the two frozen configs?
5. What evidence supports the assumption that local-harness deltas on 25 public games predict deltas on the 55 private games — is there any historical local-vs-private correlation you can compute from Preview data?
6. Is the "≥0.21 noise" figure literally from one v31/v32 pair, or do you have more repeated-identical-code submissions you did not report?
7. For the P2 kill criterion ("Class-A on <4/25 games"), what is the probability of a false kill if the true rate is 6/25, given single-run-per-game evaluation?

## Verdict: MAJOR-REVISION

## Score: 4/10

The strategic skeleton (adopt the public floor, differentiate with search/WM/TTT, gate locally) is coherent and the kill criteria show real pre-registration discipline. But the proposal's core competitive claim is measurement superiority, and the measurement plan as specified cannot distinguish its own success criteria from noise: n=2 noise estimates, n=3 variance ratios, uncorrected sequential selection, and dev-set-only gating. Fix the inference layer — noise-band characterization first, power-derived seed counts, holdout games, selection-corrected confirmation runs — and this becomes a strong plan; execute it as written and every "verified win" is unverifiable.
