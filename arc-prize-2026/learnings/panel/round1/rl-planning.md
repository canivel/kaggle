## Summary (2 sentences)

The proposal is strategically sane (adopt the dominant open baseline, differentiate via search + world models + online learning, gate everything on a local instrument), but its load-bearing search and measurement claims are under-specified in exactly the ways that historically kill model-based/exploration systems: unverified determinism assumptions behind the dedup archive, plan-through-model validity confused with replay accuracy, and a 3-seed A/B instrument that almost certainly lacks the power to resolve the effect sizes every gate depends on. As written, the plan would "verify" wins its own instrument cannot detect and deploy planners that exploit model error in precisely the unvisited states where planning matters.

## Objections

**[FATAL] The 3-seed local A/B instrument is underpowered for every gate in the plan** — The entire decision architecture (Phase 1–3 success criteria, kill criteria P1–P3, the "we can measure, they gamble" differentiator) rests on detecting +2 to +4 aggregate level deltas at 3 seeds, against a base harness whose *author could not reproduce his own score* and whose variance the team has not yet characterized. If per-game level counts have seed std anywhere near 1 level on even a third of the 25 games, a +3 aggregate delta is inside the noise floor of a 3-seed paired comparison, and the "no game regresses >1 level in ≥2/3 seeds" clause will fire false alarms constantly (with 25 games, expect several 1-level regressions by chance per sweep). This isn't a detail: an underpowered instrument converts the whole campaign into the same dice-rolling they accuse the fork swarm of, just moved to a private table. **Fix:** Phase 0 must end with a variance decomposition (per-game level std across ≥5 seeds) and an explicit power calculation setting seed counts and minimum-detectable-effects for each phase gate *before* Phase 1 begins. At $3.20/3-seed sweep, running 8–10 seeds is trivially affordable; there is no excuse for 3.

**[MAJOR] The state-dedup archive and Go-Explore frontier silently assume determinism and Markov-sufficient observations — neither is established** — Hashing the segmentation graph and marking states "visited" is only sound if (a) transitions are deterministic, (b) the segmentation graph captures all game state (no hidden counters, timers, inventory, or off-screen state), and (c) "return-to-frontier" is realizable, which in a live episodic API means replaying an action prefix and *getting the same state* — again requiring determinism. If any game violates (a) or (b), the archive prunes reachable states (aliasing) or loops on phantom frontiers; Go-Explore's published results depend entirely on simulator reset-to-state, which you do not have. **Fix:** a cheap pre-registered determinism/aliasing audit in Phase 0 — replay identical action sequences twice per game, diff frames; and check whether identical segmentation hashes ever precede divergent transitions in logged data. Gate the archive per-game on passing this audit.

**[MAJOR] "BFS instantly closes the 14 games our solver already owns" contradicts your own baseline evidence** — The v35 family, which presumably contains this BFS solver, sits at mean 0.25 with 9 games at L=0. If BFS were genuinely load-bearing on 14 games in the *live* environment, the baseline would not be at its stated structural ceiling. The likely truth: BFS solves 14 games *through your hand-built engine clones*, which is a categorically different claim — it says nothing about (i) whether the live action space (arbitrary clicks → thousands of primitive actions) is BFS-tractable without the clone, or (ii) whether the clones' correctness transfers to *private* games where no clone exists. **Fix:** state precisely what "load-bearing on 14 games" means (live API vs. clone), and report BFS branching factor / node counts on live games without the clone. If BFS only works through synthesized models, its value is fully coupled to Phase 2 succeeding, and Phase 1's success criterion is mis-attributed.

**[MAJOR] Replay accuracy is the wrong verification target for planning; the plan will exploit model error** — Phase 2's criterion (≥50% state-exact on replayed history) validates the model *on the visited distribution*, then plans with BFS *off* that distribution — the textbook model-exploitation failure mode of model-based RL. A model that is 50% state-exact on seen transitions will produce multi-step plans whose predicted trajectories diverge from reality within 1–2 steps, and BFS will preferentially find "solutions" that route through the model's optimistic errors. Rodionov's 58.12% with a frontier model does not tell you the *plan execution success rate* you'll get from a 27B's models. **Fix:** replace or augment the criterion with (a) n-step open-loop rollout accuracy on held-out trajectory suffixes, and (b) measured plan-execution success rate (fraction of planned trajectories that realize their predicted end state live), with replanning-on-contradiction as the default control loop, not an afterthought.

**[MAJOR] The TTT value net has no plausible training signal at the stated scale** — A random-init CNN trained online on (segmentation features → progress delta) inside a 2.2 h/game budget will see perhaps tens of genuinely informative progress events per game in sparse-feedback games — exactly the games it's meant to rescue. The StochasticGoose precedent is from a different harness, action space, and evaluation, and at 12.58% absolute it is weak evidence that this transfers into a Duck-based agent. Three weeks (Phase 3) is a large bet on a component with no offline validation. **Fix:** before Phase 3 begins, train the net offline on logged Phase-1/2 trajectories and report ranking correlation (e.g., Spearman between net scores and realized progress of candidate actions). If it can't rank actions offline with abundant data, it will not learn online with scarce data; make this a pre-registered entry gate for Phase 3.

**[MAJOR] Additive compounding of phase gains (+3, +4, +2 → ≥1.6) is unjustified — all three components attack the same bottleneck** — Dedup exploration, model-based planning, and value-guided action ranking all convert wasted probe actions into directed ones; their gains on any given game overlap heavily and will be strongly sub-additive. They also compete for the same 2.2 h/game wall clock, 60 s tool loop, and 32k context, so later components partially *cannibalize* earlier ones' budget. The Phase-4 target of ≥1.6 inherits this arithmetic. **Fix:** set the Phase-4 expectation from the ablation matrix's measured joint effect, not summed marginals, and pre-register a lower "still worth submitting" threshold so the freeze decision isn't anchored to an optimistic number.

**[MINOR] Restart-with-notes voting may trade RHAE efficiency for level count** — If RHAE penalizes action count (the Phase-3 criterion implies it does), restarting after stagnation burns action budget on games where the first attempt was near-threshold; "keep best level reached" is not free under an efficiency-weighted metric. Report the voting ablation in RHAE, not levels, and include the no-restart arm.

**[MINOR] Kill-criterion/success-criterion gap in Phase 2** — P2 kills at <4/25 Class-A but success requires ≥8/25 at ≥50% state-exact; the 4–7 zone has no pre-registered decision, which is exactly where a marginal result will land and where motivated reasoning lives. Close the gap.

## Questions for the authors

1. What is the measured per-game seed variance of the Duck baseline (levels, not aggregate), and what minimum detectable effect does your 3-seed paired design actually have at, say, 80% power?
2. Is "BFS load-bearing on 14 games" a claim about the live API or about your offline engine clones? What is the live branching factor after action abstraction, and what abstraction is used for clicks?
3. Have you ever replayed an identical action sequence twice on each of the 25 public games and confirmed frame-identical outcomes? Which games, if any, fail?
4. For the Phase-2 world models: what plan-execution success rate (planned vs. realized end state) did your 22 Class-A offline sims achieve when used for planning, as opposed to replay scoring?
5. How many progress-delta training events per game do Phase-1 logs actually contain for the sparse-feedback games the TTT net is supposed to rescue?
6. Does RHAE penalize total action count, and if so, what is the expected RHAE cost of one restart on a median game?
7. What happens in P2's undefined 4–7/25 Class-A zone?

## Score: 4/10

The strategic skeleton (baseline adoption, local-first gating, pre-registered kills) is better than most competition proposals I review, but the plan's spine — the measurement instrument and the search-substrate assumptions — is asserted rather than validated, and every downstream decision inherits that weakness. Fix the power analysis, the determinism audit, the plan-validity metric, and the offline TTT gate, and this becomes fundable; as written, it would pass its own gates on noise.
