## Summary (2 sentences)
The proposal is unusually well-instrumented (pre-registered kill criteria, local A/B gating, falsifiable phase gates), but its centerpiece — a runtime Rodionov verify-refactor world-model loop — rests on an unvalidated transfer claim: that offline, frontier-model, human-iterated sim-building quality (22/24 Class-A with Opus, 10 h, 7 M tokens) predicts what a 27B model can synthesize online in a 32k-context, 60 s-loop, 2.2 h/game budget. Within my domain (synthesis, world-models-as-code, verification), the verification protocol itself is Goodhart-able, the synthesis-quality metrics are under-specified, and the "general-by-construction" thesis is contradicted by the apparent game-specificity of the BFS engine-clone path.

## Objections

**[MAJOR] The "de-risked at build-quality level" claim is a category error.** The 22/24 Class-A sims were produced by claude-opus offline, with full trajectory access, unbounded iteration, and (per the budget section) 7 M tokens — none of which the in-kernel Qwen 27B has. Rodionov's 58.12% used GPT-5.5; the proposal offers zero data points on Qwen-27B-as-synthesizer, yet commits 21 slots (Jul 28–Aug 17) before the first measurement. Actionable fix: run a 3–5 game pilot on the RunPod A40 during Phase 0/1 (it's idle capacity) measuring Qwen's state-exact rate with template scaffolds; make Phase 2 entry conditional on that pilot, not the calendar.

**[MAJOR] `verify()` against replayed history is training-set accuracy and is Goodhart-able by the synthesizer.** Exact-match replay against the collected `transitions` buffer rewards a model that memorizes observed transitions — a 27B under refactor pressure will converge on if-else lookups over seen states (no game identifiers needed; the states themselves are the identifiers), which scores "Class-A" and plans nowhere. Required: a held-out transition split (verify on transitions the refactor loop never saw), an explicit degenerate-model check (identity model and pure-lookup model as scored baselines that the synthesized model must beat), and an enforcement mechanism stronger than an "MDL-style prompt," e.g., a code-size/branch-count penalty in the acceptance rule.

**[MAJOR] "State-exact" is not defined at a horizon, and the success metric is not linked to planning utility.** ≥50% state-exact could mean one-step prediction, where predicting the identity frame often scores high on ARC-like games with sparse changes, or k-step rollout, which is what BFS-through-the-model actually requires. Errors on rare, reward-relevant transitions (exactly the ones underrepresented in history) are what break planning, and a uniform exact-match average hides them. Fix: define the metric as (a) k-step rollout exact-match with k stated, and (b) **plan-transfer rate** — fraction of plans found in the model that execute successfully in the real environment — and gate Phase 2 on (b), since that is the quantity that produces levels.

**[MAJOR] The BFS path contradicts the "zero game-specific logic" thesis and inverts the dependency order.** "BFS engine-clone solver, load-bearing on 14 games" strongly implies BFS plans through hand-built per-game engine clones — i.e., it contributes on private games *only if* Phase 2's runtime synthesis succeeds in producing equivalent models there. Yet Phase 1's success criterion (+≥3 levels) will be largely paid by BFS on the 14 public games it "already owns," booking a public-set-overfit gain as evidence of a general component. Fix: report Phase 1 deltas with the 14 BFS-owned games excluded, and state explicitly what `bfs_solve()` consumes on a never-seen game (runtime WM? nothing?).

**[MAJOR] Template-library leakage into the generality claim is asserted, not audited.** The 25 sims encode structural priors of the 25 public games; using them as few-shot scaffolds imports those priors into every private-game synthesis attempt, and "no game identifiers, structure only" is exactly the kind of claim Rodionov's own audit (5 channels found) exists to distrust. Required experiment: leave-one-game-out scaffolding on the public set (seed the loop for game *i* only with templates from games ≠ *i*) — if state-exact rates drop sharply, the templates are carrying game content, not methodology. Also deliver the leakage audit as a written artifact, since it is cited as "our checklist" but never scheduled.

**[MINOR] Metric definitions are internally inconsistent and the local→LB mapping is unstated.** The Phase 2 success criterion uses "≥50% state-exact on ≥8/25" while kill criterion P2 uses "Class-A on <4/25" — if Class-A ≠ 50% state-exact, the gate and the kill can both fire; define one threshold. Separately, the Phase 4 target (≥1.6) is derived from "+9 local levels compounding," but no levels→RHAE conversion is given, so the headline success criterion is not currently falsifiable from the local instrument.

**[MINOR] P2's kill test doesn't isolate the variable it claims to confirm.** "One refactor-prompt iteration" failing would confirm "27B ceiling" only if prompt/scaffold quality were already optimized; as written it conflates synthesizer capability with harness quality. Cheap fix: pre-register 2–3 structurally distinct scaffold variants (e.g., fill-in-the-skeleton vs. free-form vs. diff-based refactor) before declaring the model ceiling reached.
