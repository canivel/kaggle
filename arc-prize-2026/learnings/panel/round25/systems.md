### NEW OBJECTIONS

### [FATAL] The free-rail screen and the scored rail are different throughput regimes, so §A's "build-rail instrument is the single most important asset" is unestablished for exactly the class of mechanism lane (a) is

§A concedes the private twin is unobservable and concludes we must measure indirectly on the free build rail. But the two rails have *different wall-clock envelopes*: the eval rail runs 25 games at `max_runtime_s_per_game=7920` completing in ~2.2h wall (prior-round read of both pulls), while §A.3 states the scored run is **9h and plays both public and private sets in the same run** — i.e. roughly twice the games in ~4× the wall time, at whatever concurrency the submission notebook uses. For a mechanism whose entire cost is per-turn latency (P1 spends wall clock inside the sandbox to buy better turns), the cost/benefit ratio is a *function of the per-game time budget*, so a P1 arm can be net-negative at 7920 s/game and net-positive at the scored rail's budget, or vice versa — and the document bets the campaign on the free-rail sign transferring. Required before the box opens, and it is free: pull the logs of one recent scored frozen-fork draw and extract per-game wallclock, concurrency, actions/game and seconds-per-action; if seconds-per-action or games-in-flight differ from the eval rail by more than ~20%, either re-configure the eval rail to match the scored regime or pre-register that latency-sensitive arms are screened at the scored rail's per-game budget. Without this, §I question 6 answers itself in the negative for lane (a) specifically.

### [MAJOR] The quota ledger is internally inconsistent by ~2×: a claimed "~9 GPU-h/day recovery" exceeds the entire claimed 30 GPU-h/week

§C5 states the rail yields 12–13 builds/week from a 30 GPU-h/week quota, and in the same sentence that "the auto-spawned save&run twin can be cancelled to recover **~9 GPU-h/day**." 9 GPU-h/day is 63 GPU-h/week — you cannot recover 2.1× the quota you claim to have. The same arithmetic problem appears from the other direction: the 33-day daily submission streak is 33 × 9h ≈ 297 GPU-h of scored compute, which is impossible under a 30 GPU-h/week ceiling, so **submission/rerun compute and interactive save&run quota are demonstrably two different currencies that this document adds together**. Fix: split the ledger explicitly into (a) competition rerun compute (does it debit the weekly quota at all? — verify against the Kaggle account's usage page, not from memory), (b) interactive/save&run GPU-h, and (c) the save&run twin, and restate Option 4's cost in the (b) currency only. Until that is done, "≈12–13 builds/week" and "1–2 pushes at most" are not commensurable numbers and Option 4 is unpriced.

### [MAJOR] Cancelling the save&run twin is the mechanism by which every screen's artifacts are produced — the "recovery" may delete the pull path

Every screen in this plan depends on pulling `benchmark.json`, `summary.txt`, transcripts and the solver banner from a *committed kernel output version*; the interactive session's ephemeral state is not what `runs/kernel_pulls/*` contains. If the "auto-spawned save&run twin" is the run that produces the committed output, cancelling it to recover GPU-h destroys the artifact that K3′, K4 and the canary all read. Before that recovery is booked into the budget, run one throwaway build with the twin cancelled and confirm that the full pull set is still retrievable; if it is not, delete the ~9 GPU-h/day line from §C5 entirely, which further tightens NEW-3's already-inconsistent ledger.

### [MAJOR] Option 2's "GLM-4.6V-AWQ (106B MoE) fits 96GB" is a weights-only claim and ignores KV cache at concurrency 25–28, engine-start overhead, and vLLM architecture support

"Fits 96GB" prices the parameters and nothing else. The binding quantity on this rail is **weights + KV cache × concurrent sequences × context length + vision-tower activations**; our duck runs ~25 games in flight with long contexts, and at 4-bit a 106B MoE leaves on the order of 30–40 GB for everything else — which may or may not hold 25 concurrent long-context sequences, and the document offers no bytes-per-token-per-layer arithmetic to say which. Three further unpriced systems costs: (i) a ~55–60 GB weight artifact must be uploaded to a Kaggle dataset and read into the kernel before vLLM init, and that fixed overhead eats the same 2.2h build window that produced A17's `t=720s` freeze confound; (ii) the kernel image's pinned vLLM version must already register this architecture *and* a matching native-FC tool-call parser, offline, with no network — if it does not, you are installing a wheel, which is a new build class and re-opens the structural-drift ERROR family `preflight.py` exists to block; (iii) MoE decode throughput at high concurrency is a different curve from dense-72B and must be measured, not assumed better. Pre-register, before the screen: total VRAM budget arithmetic, target tokens/s at the observed concurrency, the implied actions/game, and a kill line analogous to A17's ρ_action ≥ 3.5.

### [MAJOR] K3′'s m ≥ 3 baseline requirement is priced in prose but its statistical power is never priced in GPU-hours — the box may buy a screen that cannot resolve any plausible effect

§C4 sets PASS at `mean Δlc ≥ −t(0.95, m−1)·s_base·√(1+1/m)`; at m=3 that is `−3.37·s_base`, and §C3 tells us the warpack-family `s_base` is 4.83× vanilla's. The document has therefore replaced a gate with a 51% false-FAIL rate by one whose false-PASS behaviour is completely unstated — and the fix for that is more baseline runs, each costing 2.2–2.4 GPU-h out of the ledger that NEW-3 shows is already inconsistent. This is a compute-economics question, not just a statistics one: state the **minimum detectable effect** in Δlc at m=3, m=5 and m=10 for both the vanilla and warpack nulls, convert each to GPU-h, and check whether the MDE at the affordable m is smaller than any effect lane (a) could plausibly produce. If the affordable MDE exceeds the plausible effect, the honest conclusion is that P1 is unscreenable on this rail and §I question 2 must be answered "no" before the 3-week box is opened.

### [MINOR] The 3-week box should be denominated in build-days, not weeks, and it is tighter than it looks

The critical path is serial: instrument fixes (offline) → m≥3 baselines → P1 arm → read next session → seed-2 → optional VOID re-push, plus a parallel GLM track that contends for the same concurrent-session slot, not just the same quota. At one build per session with the read landing the following day, that is ≥6 working days of the 21 before any K4 reading exists, and one K1/K2 VOID or one vLLM-init failure on the GLM leg costs a full day each. Restate Option 4's box as an explicit build-day Gantt with named slack, and pre-register what gets dropped first when the box slips (my recommendation: the GLM leg, since its failure mode is the one with the least diagnostic value for the private twin).
