### Part 2 — new objections

**N1 [FATAL] The campaign-concede trigger is an undefined scalar that the panel has already ruled can only fail invalidly.** Option 4 pre-commits: "if K4 fails and the GLM screen format-livelocks, we adopt Option 3" — i.e. `namespace_reuse_rate < 0.15` retires the campaign's only path to the prize. Yet §E.5 (llm-agents FATAL, accepted as policy) states the harness tells the model its Python is ephemeral in ~6 places that §6 keeps, so a sub-0.15 reading measures instruction conflict; and §E.5 additionally establishes that `SAFE_MODULES` lacks `dataclasses`/`typing`/`enum`, so the target artifact **cannot be constructed at all** — the metric is guaranteed low for reasons unrelated to 27B's capability. Pre-committing a campaign-level off-ramp to a statistic with a known, documented, uncorrected invalid-failure mode is decision-theoretically indefensible. Required before any seal: (a) the prompt/tool-schema strings promoted to patch surface and the safe modules added, so the substrate is actually available; (b) `namespace_reuse_rate` defined operationally with a *load-bearing* variant (name defined ≥2 turns earlier, referenced in a call that emits an action); (c) the 0.15 floor derived from the observed cross-turn-reference distribution in existing `python`-tool transcripts, reported per-game across 25 games, not pooled; (d) a stated power calculation — with ~67 turns/game, what is the sampling s.d. of the per-game rate, and what true rate does a 0.15 threshold discriminate against at 80% power?

**N2 [FATAL] The in-scope subset excludes the components that carry Tycho's measured lift, so lane (a) as scoped does not test the architecture hypothesis.** §E states plainly that the builder subagent, `/refine` self-editing, and `rlm()` sub-agents are all out of scope (52× LM-call gap), leaving P1 (persistent namespace) + P3 (un-wipe) — i.e. **memory substrate with no policy-improvement operator**. In model-based RL terms you are porting the model store and omitting planning, model repair, and the action-selection criterion. Tycho's own H2 result, which the document cites approvingly in §G, is precisely the evidence that the store is *necessary and not sufficient* (88.1% transition match, still lost on completions). Required: a causal decomposition of Tycho's reported gain — specifically, which component the fixed-model ablation's **+9.4 RHAE** is attributed to (§H Option 1 quotes that number as the lever) — and a statement of what fraction of it, if any, is attributable to the in-scope subset. If the answer is "unknown" or "the loop," then Option 4's EV must be restated as "we are testing whether 27B uses a variable," which is worth running but is not "the only lever that can move the private twin."

**N3 [MAJOR] The gold target is wrong by roughly the size of the lift being debated.** §0.1 and §G assert gold drifts "**+~0.01/day**" and projects "**~1.6** by Nov 2." Eighty-five days at +0.01/day is **+0.85 → ~2.4**. Even the document's own conservative observable (1.49 on 07-28 → 1.58 on 08-09 = +0.0075/day) projects **~2.2**. The "~1.6" figure implies near-total flattening, which may well be right — an order statistic of a saturating field should flatten — but that model is asserted nowhere. This is load-bearing: the required lift over our 1.33 is either **+0.27** or **+0.9**, and the second number probably kills Option 1 on its own. Required: fit the top-13 line to a saturating form (e.g. logistic or log-time) over the archived `runs/lb_daily/*.csv` series, publish the fitted Nov-2 point with an interval, and re-run §G's viability question against the interval's *upper* end, not a round number.

**N4 [MAJOR] "Per-draw MEAN is the currency" is the wrong conclusion from §A's own premises, and no selection rule exists.** §A establishes that every run banks a joint (public, private) pair and that we select **two** submissions on Nov 2. That is still a selection problem over an order statistic — we simply cannot observe the coordinate we want. The correct statement is not "mean is the currency"; it is "the value of additional draws is governed by ρ(public, private), and our selection rule must be evaluated as a function of ρ." At ρ≈0, selecting the two best public draws returns E[private] ≈ mean and extra draws are worthless (the document's claim). At ρ high — plausible, since both sets are played in the *same run* under the *same* latency/vLLM conditions, and §A itself notes they are correlated "through same-run luck" — public luck-harvesting pays substantially. Required: (i) state the Nov-2 selection rule explicitly (which two submissions, chosen on what observable, decided now not in October); (ii) a sensitivity table of E[selected private] under ρ ∈ {0, 0.3, 0.6, 0.9}; (iii) any available same-run public-vs-private consistency evidence (e.g. do our per-game public level-completions correlate with run-level conditions like `final_wallclock_seconds` / vLLM readiness across the 26 draws — that is a cheap ρ-surrogate we already own).

**N5 [MAJOR] K3′'s type-I fix has no reported type-II, and on its own numbers it may be a gate that passes everything.** §C4 replaces a 51%-false-FAIL gate with a paired-mean rule at m≥3. But §C3's baseline family is 22/15/13 levels — a per-game s_base large enough that `−t(0.95, df=2)·s_base·√(1+1/3)` is a very permissive line, which is exactly why "v1 +0.013, v2 −0.107, v2.1 −0.147 — **all PASS**." A gate that retroactively passes all three strikes of a lane you still decline to fund is not obviously calibrated; it may simply be powerless. Required, and computable from `runs/null10` today at zero GPU cost: the **power curve of K3′** against stipulated true harms of −0.10 / −0.20 / −0.40 mean Δlc at m = 1, 3, 5, plus the same for the warpack-variance family (4.83× vanilla).
