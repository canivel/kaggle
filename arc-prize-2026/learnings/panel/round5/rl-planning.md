## Summary (2 sentences)
The proposal is a substantial improvement over v4 in epistemic hygiene — pre-registered gates, paired null10 controls, and honest kill criteria — but its two workhorse components rest on internally contradictory evidence: the compute-reallocation levers (R1) assume marginal compute on progressing games is valuable, while the proposal's own headline finding ("STUCK, not out-of-time"; 9/126 runs progressing at the end) says it is nearly worthless. The R2 level-2 attack, the only lever big enough to matter, hides an under-specified search proposal behind a gate whose statistics ignore within-game seed correlation.

## Objections

**[FATAL] R1's EV contradicts the proposal's own Finding #3 —** You proved that "extra budget buys little" because runs die STUCK: only 9/126 good runs were still progressing in the final 15% of wall. L1 frees ~20% of compute and reallocates it to progressing games, but if those games plateau before the wall (which your data says they do), the marginal value of reallocated compute is ≈0, and the claimed EV of +0.10–0.30 has no derivation reconciling this. The same logic guts L2's +0.13: a fresh episode helps only if failure is within-episode variance, yet your 10/10-seed death data says these failures are deterministic per game. **Required evidence before R1 ships:** a counterfactual replay on the null10 per-action event logs — truncate dead games at action 120, extend progressing games with the freed budget, and report the simulated Δ with CIs. You have this corpus; the analysis is free and takes a day. If simulated Δ < +0.1, R1 is not worth 2–3 windows and the whole +0.25 "levers" line in the target arithmetic evaporates.

**[MAJOR] L1/L2 threshold interaction is incoherent as specified —** L2 restarts at action 90 on `lc==0`; L1 deprioritizes at action 120 on `lc==0`. If the action counter resets on restart (unspecified), dead games restart in a loop at 90 and *never reach* the 120 deprioritization trigger — the 5 dead games eating 20.2% of compute would then eat *more*, not less. If the counter does not reset, L2 fires exactly once and L1 kills the game 30 actions later, making L2 nearly vacuous. Specify counter semantics, restart caps per game, and the firing order, and show the combined policy's behavior on the null10 dead-game transcripts before the build.

**[MAJOR] The R2 primary gate's independence assumption is false and inflates its claimed selectivity —** "≥3/9 clears, P ≈ 0.05" treats 3 seeds × 3 games as 9 independent Bernoulli trials, but your own data shows clears are near-deterministic per game (10/10 or 0/10 at level 1). Under game-level clustering the effective n is 3, and one game flipping to "solvable" yields 3/9 by itself — the gate passes on a single-game fix, which is exactly the implicit game-specific overfit your "no game-ID logic" rule is meant to prevent, and the false-positive probability under the null of "one lucky game-level artifact" is far above 0.05. Fix: require clears on **≥2 distinct games** of {sb26, su15, lp85}, or state the gate as a per-game hierarchical test. This is one line to change and it is load-bearing for the only $-spend in the plan.

**[MAJOR] "Duck+BFS hybrid over the object graph" is an under-specified search claim —** BFS requires a state space, an action model, a transition function, and a goal test; none is specified, and ARC-AGI-3 exposes no forward model to the agent. If "BFS" means executing real environment actions systematically, it is breadth-first *exploration* paying the quadratic RHAE wallclock penalty you just proved kills score (−0.33 levels from +25–46% tokens/action); if it means search over a learned/heuristic object-graph model, you have zero evidence the duck can induce a transition model at level 2 — indeed your forensics question ("does the agent ever state the correct mechanic?") suggests it cannot. Before the Aug 3 deliverable, each shortlist item must specify: what is simulated vs executed, cost in actions and generated tokens per node expanded, and the predicted tokens/action delta against the >10% kill criterion. Otherwise R2 will burn the $68 on a re-branded version of the exploration machinery this same proposal just killed.

**[MAJOR] The window gate is uninterpretable at the admitted σ̂ upper endpoint —** With σ̂ ∈ [0.044, 0.213] (df=5), the promote threshold +0.12 is 2.0 SE at the point estimate but only ~0.7 SE at the upper endpoint; "printing the CI beside every decision" is not a decision rule. Meanwhile kill-at-Δ<0 on 2 draws false-kills a true +0.1 effect ~30% of the time by your own admission, and most of your realistic candidates (+0.14 official from levers) sit *at* the promote threshold. Fix concretely: (a) spend R0 windows to get σ̂ to df≥9 before any candidate gate, and (b) pre-register what happens when the decision flips sign across the CI endpoints (currently undefined).

**[MAJOR] The Nov-2 thesis — "game-agnostic logic compresses the luck-chasers" — is asserted with zero evidence —** This assumption is load-bearing: it is what makes a Sep-30 top-100 checkpoint an acceptable formal objective rather than a concession of the campaign. You provide no data that rivals above rank 100 are overfit to the public set (e.g., inspection of public notebooks for game-ID-keyed logic, or public/private score-gap statistics from ARC-AGI-1/2 analogues). Supply at minimum: an audit of the public Milestone-1 winning notebook for game-specific heuristics, and a historical estimate of public→private rank compression from prior ARC competitions. If that evidence is weak, the whole risk-acceptance in Risk #1 is unjustified and the plan needs a genuinely bigger Sep swing.

**[MINOR] Exploration is closed as a *family* when only one implementation was falsified —** The replicated harm (ar25, su15, p<0.01) is traced to context pollution — an implementation artifact — not to exploration as a mechanism, and your own level-2 forensics (hypothesis churn, never stating the mechanic) point toward *insufficient directed exploration* as the capability gap. Closing the injection family until Nov after one gate + one retry of a single variant conflates the mechanism with its delivery vehicle. Cheap fix: add to the R2 forensics a state-coverage metric at level 2 (distinct object-graph states visited per 100 actions, null vs stuck), so the "capability wall = exploration failure" hypothesis is tested rather than foreclosed.

## Questions for the authors

2. Does the L2 restart reset the action counter used by L1, and is there a per-game restart cap? What does the combined L1+L2 policy do on the five 10/10-dead games, replayed on null10?

6. What evidence supports "private-set compression of overfit rivals"? Have you audited the public winning notebook for game-ID-keyed logic?
