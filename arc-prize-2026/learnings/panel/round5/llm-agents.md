## Summary (2 sentences)
The proposal makes a genuinely evidence-driven subtractive turn (kill always-on injection, reallocate compute, gate everything), and its process discipline is above the bar for this kind of campaign. However, its entire win condition rests on R2 — a level-2 capability intervention specified only as a three-item shortlist of scaffold names — while the cheapest available evidence source (the public Milestone-1 winning notebook, run by a leader at 1.56 vs. their 1.02) is never mentioned, and the plan's primary free lever (reallocation) is contradicted by the plan's own "STUCK, not out-of-time" finding.

## Objections

**[MAJOR] The public winning Milestone-1 notebook is entirely absent from the plan** — The panel brief states the winning notebook is public and the leader sits at 1.56 against your best draw of 1.02. Nowhere in the proposal is there a plan to run that notebook on the null10 corpus, diff its harness against the duck (context-management policy, tool surface, per-game scheduling, restart logic), and port the deltas through your own window gate. This is the single cheapest headroom experiment available — it costs CPU and one or two windows, not $68 — and it directly tests whether the level-2 wall is a wall for *your scaffold* or for *the model class*. A plan that spends its only GPU dollars on a speculative in-house intervention while ignoring a public artifact demonstrably 0.5+ ahead has misordered its evidence acquisition. Fix: add a mandatory R0/R1-adjacent "SOTA differencing" task with a concrete deliverable (harness diff table + which deltas are game-agnostic and portable).

**[MAJOR] R2's intervention shortlist is exactly the hand-waving the rest of the document forbids** — "Duck+BFS hybrid over the object graph," "stall-scoped exec-WM verify loop," and "capability router" are three names, not three designs: no I/O spec, no statement of what each assumes about the failure mode, no engineering estimate for the Jul 21–Aug 10 window. Worse, the forensics deliverable ("does the agent ever state the correct mechanic?") has no pre-registered decision mapping: if the agent *never* verbalizes the mechanic in 30 grinds, that is a model-capability wall and no verify-loop or prompt restructure will conjure the mechanic — only a search/solver hybrid (which is a real build, weeks of work) has a chance; if the agent *does* state it and loses it, that is a context-management bug and BFS is the wrong tool. And note the tension: all three shortlist items are context *injections*, in a proposal whose thesis is "inject never-by-default." Fix: pre-register the forensics→intervention decision table before Aug 3 (observable signature → intervention class → falsifiable prediction), and give each shortlist item a one-page design with token-overhead budget against the >10% tokens/action kill rule.

**[MAJOR] The reallocator's EV contradicts the proposal's own Evidence #3** — L1's case is "free ~20% of compute for progressing games, EV +0.10–0.30," but the same document proves that only 9/126 good runs were still progressing in the final 15% of wall and that the median good run burns 59% of tokens grinding a level it never finishes. If recipient games are STUCK rather than out-of-time, the marginal freed token buys approximately nothing, and L1's true EV is near zero (L2's restart at least attacks stuckness directly). The +0.10–0.30 figure is asserted, not measured. Fix: before spending windows on R1, run an offline counterfactual replay on null10 — reallocate the 20.2% dead-game budget per L1's rule and estimate marginal level-clear probability from the empirical time-to-clear distributions; if the replay EV is <+0.10, ship L2-only.

**[MAJOR] L1/L2 interaction semantics are unspecified and can thrash on the five dead games** — L2 restarts any game with `lc==0` at action 90; the five 10/10-dead games will hit that trigger *every episode*. Do restarts reset the action counter L1 reads at 120? If yes, dead games loop restart→90 actions→restart indefinitely and L2 *destroys* the exact compute L1 exists to reclaim; the combined build then fails its 2-window gate for a reason that is a bug, not a hypothesis, burning the family's only retry. Fix: specify precedence (L1 deprioritization dominates), cumulative-across-episodes counters, and a max-restarts-per-game cap, and smoke-test the interaction on null10 replay before the first window.

**[MAJOR] The R2 primary gate does not test generalization, but the Nov-2 thesis requires it** — "≥3/9 level-2 clears on {sb26, su15, lp85}" pooled can be satisfied by 3/3 on one game and 0/6 on the others — i.e., an intervention functionally overfit to one public game's mechanic (a BFS-over-object-graph that happens to match sb26's structure), which then ships to ~55 unseen private games under a thesis that explicitly bets on game-agnostic transfer. The "no game-ID-keyed logic" rule does not prevent functional overfitting. Fix: require clears on ≥2 distinct games as the primary gate, or hold one grinder out of intervention development entirely and require ≥1 clear on it.

**[MINOR] Window-gate baseline staleness under proven drift** — Promotion authority is a 2-window mean vs. a 6-draw baseline mean, but 15/24 games were version-unstable in July and the drift sentinel is weekly with no stated rule for what invalidates or refreshes the 6-draw control. A drifted environment makes every gated Δ a difference against a stale distribution; a candidate could promote (or die) on drift alone. Fix: pre-register a baseline-refresh trigger (e.g., sentinel deviates >2σ̂ → the next N sentinel draws replace the control mean). I flag also that the power analysis at the upper σ̂ CI endpoint (SE ≈ 0.16, making +0.12 < 1 SE) belongs to the statistics reviewer, and I defer the depth of that critique to them.

## Questions for the authors
1. What is the "duck" concretely — base model, context budget, tool surface — and have you run the public Milestone-1 winning notebook (or a faithful reimplementation) on any subset of null10? If not, why is that experiment not in the plan?
2. In the existing null10 transcripts of sb26/su15/lp85 level-2 grinds, does the agent ever verbalize the correct mechanic? You already have this data — the answer determines which R2 intervention class is even viable, so why is it deferred to Aug 3?
3. L1/L2 semantics: do L2 restarts reset the counters L1 reads? What is the max-restart cap per game? Which lever has precedence on the five dead games?
4. Where, specifically, does L1's freed 20% of compute land, and what is the measured marginal value of additional actions on a game currently mid-grind, given your own finding that death mode is STUCK?
5. Will the R2 primary gate be amended to require level-2 clears on ≥2 distinct games? If not, what protects the Nov-2 generalization thesis from a single-game-overfit intervention?
6. What sentinel deviation triggers a refresh of the 6-draw baseline used by every window gate?
7. What evidence supports "private-set compression of overfit rivals" — have you measured any rival's public-vs-consistency signature (e.g., score volatility across their draws), or is this asserted?
