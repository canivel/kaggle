"3/3 dataset_sources survived" checks *attachment*, not *loadability*. **[MAJOR] OQ3 is posed but the answer is computable now, and nobody computed it — at the +0.17-class prior, REFUTE is likely uninterpretable.** Using the brief's own numbers: a true +0.17 lift shifts the draw mean to ≈1.11; a mean-of-4 at s=0.1563 gives σ/√4≈0.078, so even a *real* +0.17 effect clears the 1.0826 bar only ~64% of the time, and the sealed CONFIRM/REFUTE thresholds are in *levels* (≥32 / ≤25) with no stated mapping from +0.17 LB-points to Δlevels or its variance. If MDE > +0.17-class, pre-commit now that REFUTE-2× is logged as UNDERPOWERED-AT-PRIOR, not as evidence against the engine — otherwise the arm burns 2+ slots to produce a result the brief has already predicted and cannot interpret.

**[MAJOR] The `"$PYTHON" - <<'PYSETUP'` heredoc surface has now killed kernels twice (A17 07-25, LoRA 08-14) and the fix shipped is to the fingerprint *store*, not the *pattern*.** The postmortem correctly identifies that a separate interpreter cannot see notebook-cell names and that `compile()`-only checks are structurally blind to this class — then the remediation list (backfill-before-report, staleness banner, `--dry-run`) improves *detection after death*. The prevention fix is cheap and specific: (a) lint rule rejecting any heredoc body that references a name defined in notebook cells, or (b) require the build check to *execute* the heredoc in an isolated interpreter with the same visibility, not merely compile it.
