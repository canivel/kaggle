## Summary (2 sentences)
This brief is unusually disciplined about single-draw noise in its own ledger (the 1.17 draw is correctly discounted, the promotion bar is correctly re-read, not cached), but it then commits the exact sin it polices when reading *other teams'* data: it re-anchors the Q38 prior to "+0.17-class" from two censored single draws while excluding a third dateable observation (+0.74) reported in the same section, and it treats a two-team, one-draw-each null as "the load-bearing observation" against the engine-swap story without any power statement. The operational content (Q38 v2 push, instrument-defect fixes, fingerprint backfill) is sound; the inferential layer feeding Open Questions 3–4 needs repair before the sealed read lands.

## Objections

[MAJOR] The "+0.17-class" re-anchor is a selective, downward-biased estimator drawn from n=2 — while n=3 is on the page. Section 1b's own attribution finding establishes a *third* dateable post-release draw: `aRc (binary relation)`, 1.17 → 1.91 (**+0.74**) on exactly one submission, by the team whose member wrote "we are currently using qwen 3.6" — the same evidence class (ΔSub==1) as Ya Xu (+0.17) and FOYSAL (+0.00). Re-anchoring to "+0.17-class" silently drops the largest observation from the identical dateability criterion; the honest statement is "dateable effects span 0.00 to +0.74, n=3, heterogeneity dominates the estimate." Separately, all three are censored: a public score is a MAX, so for a team whose incumbent best sits high in its own draw distribution (FOYSAL at 1.61), a true mean shift of +0.17 frequently produces Δ=0.00 in one draw — the estimator is biased toward zero by the very best-of-N mechanism §1a lectures about. Fix: state the prior as a wide interval with the censoring direction noted, not a point class, before the sealed read returns.

[MAJOR] The "control arm did not move" claim has near-zero power and must not be labeled load-bearing. Jack Cole and Tufa Labs each made **one** new submission against incumbent bests (1.59, 1.62) that are themselves high order statistics of their own processes. Under a draw s of ~0.15 (our measured scale for this artifact family), P(one new draw fails to beat a high incumbent max | true +0.17 mean shift) is plausibly 0.4–0.7 per team; two joint failures is entirely unremarkable, so Δ/draw = 0.0000 × 2 discriminates almost nothing between "no shared engine lift" and "+0.17 lift present." Calling this "the opposite of what a shared commodity-engine story predicts" converts an underpowered null into affirmative evidence — the exact error class of 2608.13087, which the brief itself ADOPTs as a lens. Fix: either compute the actual detection probability under the +0.17 hypothesis (a two-line calculation from the ledger s) and quote it next to the claim, or demote the observation from "load-bearing" to "consistent with, uninformative."

[MAJOR] Open Question 3 is posed correctly but cannot be answered without a power statement, and none exists. The panel is asked to decide *now* whether REFUTE-2× at a +0.17-class true effect refutes the engine claim or our detection power — but no one has stated P(REFUTE | true effect = +0.17), P(REFUTE | δ = 0), or the variance of the level-count endpoint under the sealed thresholds (CONFIRM ≥32 / REFUTE ≤25). Without those numbers the pre-commitment is theater: the panel would be choosing a label, not a decision rule. Fix (no unsealing required): simulate or bound the level-count distribution from existing control-run telemetry / the frozen-fork record, publish P(CONFIRM), P(REFUTE), P(inconclusive) under δ ∈ {0, +0.17, +0.74} *before* the kernel completes, and let the disposition follow mechanically.

[MINOR] The band-median argument (+0.01, "movement into the band, not a lift of it") inherits the same max-censoring defect. Incumbent public scores cannot decrease and can only increase by beating their own max, so a shared +0.17 mean lift would move the incumbent median only slowly and stochastically over multiple draws; one day of median +0.01 with 46-sub entrants diluting the pool is weak evidence either way. The brief flags the composition artifact for submission counts but not for the score median itself. Quote it as directional color, not corroboration.

[MINOR] "s rose 0.1533 → 0.1563, which is exactly why the bar exists" — adding a +1.53σ draw mechanically inflates s; this is not a finding about dispersion, it is arithmetic. Strike the causal framing; the mean-of-4 bar is justified by the full record, not by today's increment.

[MINOR] Open Question 1's proposed CPU-only monotonicity analysis is retrospective on trajectories already on disk and therefore HARKing-prone. If adopted, it needs a mini-prereg first: define the monotonicity statistic and its null band on a nominated *subset* of trajectories (or via a permutation null), then score the held-out remainder — otherwise a "non-monotone!" finding selected from many possible statistics on old data is uninterpretable. This is cheap to do and should be a condition of adoption. Similarly, the OQ2 pre-registration of "context fix will null" is good practice but must specify the two-sided decision rule (what observed effect would count as *refuting* 2608.12321's transfer to our substrate), or it becomes an unfalsifiable told-you-so.
