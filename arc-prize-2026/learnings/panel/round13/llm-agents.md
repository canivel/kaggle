## Objections

**[MAJOR] The build rail cannot evaluate the top-ranked war-v3 candidates — the gate design will produce false negatives by construction.** The brief itself concedes ("Tension worth the panel's eyes") that the build rail forces an offline-bench regime while banking/recovery conditions only differ under the LB's full 8h budget. Yet the two candidates the author leans hardest on — (a) budget sentinel and (g) per-game budget re-allocation — are *budget-regime* mechanisms whose firing conditions may literally never occur on the offline rail, exactly as the ledger's trigger never fired (0/1552). If A1-style gates for war-v3 run on the same rail, you will re-enact the warpack ambiguity: gate-FAIL on a bench where the mechanism cannot express itself, +0.06 unpowered on LB, and no decision-grade evidence either way. Before any war-v3 gate is sealed, the rail must reproduce budget pressure (compressed wall-clock/action budget scaled to trigger sentinel and reallocation events at measurable frequency), or budget-dependent mechanisms need a separately pre-registered budget-faithful bench. This must be fixed in the gate spec *before* build, not discovered at the next gate look.

**[MAJOR] The author's lean bundles (a)+(f) into a "single window," violating the stated one-flag-per-window rule and destroying attribution.** Q1's own preamble says "one flag per window, simplicity-wins prior," then the lean proposes shipping budget sentinel and the game-over-continuation fix together. If the window gates positive you cannot tell which mechanism earned it; if it gates negative you cannot tell which to kill — this is precisely the composition ambiguity that just cost you the warpack line, where banking+recovery+retry_guard died as an unseparable bundle. Calling (f) "free hygiene" does not exempt it: it changes prompt-level control flow at game-over states, which is behaviorally load-bearing on any game with deadlock dynamics. Either ship (f) alone first as a hygiene patch with its own quick screen, or formally amend the one-flag rule with panel sign-off.

**[MAJOR] Candidate (g)'s "starve stable negatives" targeting is built on a category error.** cd82/cn04/sk48 are stable negatives *in Δ of warpack-v1 vs. null* — a mechanism the gate just refuted. A negative delta for a dead mechanism says nothing about a game's absolute convertibility under the null harness; you could starve games that are actually mid-pack contributors to per-draw mean. Budget re-allocation needs its own targeting evidence: absolute per-game clear rates and marginal-return curves (clears vs. actions spent) under the current null harness, extractable from existing run artifacts at zero new cost. Rank (g) only after that table exists.

**[MAJOR] The panel is being asked to rank the wrong backlog: the brief's own reach analysis says none of Q1 closes the wall, and the only named wall-closer has zero design content.** Order-stats ceiling is 1.11@k=110; the wall is ≥1.44; the Instruments section states grinder cracking (R3–R5) is "the ONLY live budgeted wall-closer" with build work due NLT Jul 20 — *three days from this panel* — yet Q1 contains seven context-management micro-mechanisms and not one grinder-cracking design decision for the panel to review. Every Q1 candidate also lacks even a coarse expected-Δ-per-draw-mean estimate, so "rank the backlog" is ranking on vibes plus a simplicity prior. Supply per-candidate mean-impact estimates (from distill traces: how many deaths/wasted actions would each mechanism plausibly convert, times per-clear value), and put the grinder scoping in front of this panel before Jul 20 or explain what body reviews it.

**[MAJOR] The su15 "info-theoretic wall" verdict is confounded by the very bug candidate (f) describes — Q4's "skip" lean is premature.** Per (f), the harness computed the correct restart every turn on su15 and *never executed it*. Any probe run through that harness — including the GPT-5.6 decomposition underwriting the wall verdict — measured the model against a broken control path, not against the game's information structure. "Accept the wall" is load-bearing (it removes a game from the convertible set permanently), and the disambiguating experiment costs exactly the $10 already topped up: fix (f), re-probe su15 once. Do not close this question on confounded evidence.

**[MINOR] Reviving the ledger via firing triggers (d) assumes the ledger's *content* is useful, which the negative screen never tested.** The 0-escalation result establishes the trigger never fires; it cannot distinguish "good FACTs, bad trigger" from "worthless FACTs."
