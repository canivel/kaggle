## 0. The hypothesis, as briefed

Two mechanisms have now failed identically: **P1 mechanism C** (hand the agent
the ground truth it already holds — 96.3% delivered) and **EFFNOTE** (hand it
the scoring rule + live action count + stall alarm — 93.8% delivered). Both
landed inside the block-free control spread. *The agent does not act on
runner-supplied context.*

EFFNOTE did measure one thing that looked actionable without the agent's
consent:

| | ARM (effnote_v1) | control spread |
|---|---:|---|
| **B4** actions per **stall** turn | **11.11** | 3.95 – 7.28 |
| **B4c** actions per non-stall turn | 3.92 | 3.46 – 3.94 |

The brief's reading: on exactly the turns telling it to commit to the shortest
sequence, the agent **batched harder** — long blind batches, the same object as
the 17.6% blind-batch tail of the 08-12 diagnosis. **P2 does not ask; P2 caps.**

**Mechanism as briefed:** when the runner's own stall detectors are active for
the turn, **hard-cap the number of actions executed from that turn's batch
(cap = 1..2)**, aborting the remainder and returning control to the model.

**The brief's own hard stop, quoted:** *"if the cap deletes any [level-completing
action], redesign … If the replay shows the cap cuts even one level-completing
action, DO NOT PUSH — report and redesign."*

## 1. FINDING 1 — THE HARD CAP IS REFUSED. It deletes level completions, and its apparent gain is an artefact.

`p2_replay.py` replays 30 candidate rules over **four** recorded runs
(`animation_v1`, `a22_v2_seed1`, `a22_compaction_v1`, and `effnote_v1` — the
very run B4 = 11.11 was measured on), driving the **shipped** detectors and the
**shipped** `should_abort_remainder` predicate, and scoring with the **exact**
scorer `scripts/phase1_gate.py:rhae_score`.

| rule | **LC cut** | BC cut | saved | saved% | mult | **mult (LC-honest)** | verdict |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| **cap=1** (as briefed) | **5** | 1181 | 1611 | 8.80% | 1.0574 | **0.9457** | REFUSED |
| **cap=2** (as briefed) | **5** | 962 | 1375 | 7.51% | 1.0349 | **0.9397** | REFUSED |
| cap=3 | 4 | 842 | 1235 | 6.75% | 1.0253 | 0.9487 | REFUSED |
| cap=4 | 3 | 710 | 1088 | 5.95% | 1.0173 | 0.9440 | REFUSED |
| cap=6 | 2 | 583 | 926 | 5.06% | 1.0071 | 0.9572 | REFUSED |
| abort-on-1st-revisit (`stale=1`) | 4 | 789 | 1204 | 6.58% | 1.0379 | 0.9612 | REFUSED (= `P1_ABORT_REVISIT`, already refused 08-12) |

**The `mult` column is the number a careless replay would publish, and it is a
lie.** It credits the cap with removing actions from a level *that the same cap
just prevented from completing*. The **LC-honest** column re-scores the affected
level (and every deeper one) as not completed. **Every lc-cutting rule is net
NEGATIVE — cap=1 goes ×1.0574 → ×0.9457.** The hard cap does not trade level
completions for efficiency; it loses on both.

*(This correction is now a standing rule: any replay of a mechanism that can cut
a level-completing action must publish the LC-honest multiplier alongside the
naive one. `p2_replay.py` emits both by construction.)*

### 1.1 Exactly which batches it destroys — and why every cap fails

The brief named three. The replay finds those three and one more, and they share
one structural property:

| run | game | batch size | position of the level-completing action | gate at issue |
|---|---|---:|---:|---|
| animation_v1 | **tu93** L1 | 16 | **16 (last)** | `nz=51` |
| animation_v1 | **ar25** L1 | 15 | **15 (last)** | `nz=110` |
| animation_v1 | **sp80** L1 | 4 | **4 (last)** | `nz=19,rev=4` |
| effnote_v1 | **sp80** L1 | 6 | **6 (last)** | `nz=31` |

**In all four, the completing action is the LAST action of a long,
stall-gated batch.** The batch is stall-gated precisely because it *opens with
re-traversal* — the agent walks back across ground it has already covered and
then finishes the level. Any cap at position *N* destroys every winning batch
longer than *N*, and ar25/tu93 need *N* ≥ 15. **The long stall-gated batch is
not where the waste lives; it is where the level completions live.**

## 2. FINDING 2 — THE PREMISE DOES NOT SURVIVE CONTACT WITH THE HARNESS. There is no 11-action blind batch.

B4 = 11.11 is **actions per ANALYSIS TURN**. A cap can only cap a **BATCH** —
one `step_env` call, one tool call. Those are not the same object, and the gap
between them is the whole hypothesis:

| run | batches | **size-1 batches** | median batch | mean batch | batches ≥5 | actions in batches ≥5 | **batches per analysis turn** |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| animation_v1 | 3150 | **83.6%** | 1 | 1.64 | 5.0% | 29.9% | 2.61 |
| a22_v2_seed1 | 1591 | **79.1%** | 1 | 2.20 | 10.4% | 50.8% | 1.60 |
| a22_compaction_v1 | 2540 | **85.4%** | 1 | 1.88 | 6.6% | 42.9% | 2.03 |
| effnote_v1 | 2272 | **85.4%** | 1 | 2.15 | 7.9% | 51.3% | 2.11 |

The agent reaches ~11 actions on a stall turn by making **2–4 sequential tool
calls of ~2–4 actions each** — and **the harness returns the resulting board to
it after every one of them** (`step_env` → payload → next tool call). It is not
firing 11 actions blind into a board it cannot see. It is looking at the board
between every burst and choosing to keep going.

That reclassifies the target. "Blind batching" is the wrong name for it; the
agent is **persisting deliberately across informed observations**. A batch cap
is the wrong instrument for that, because the model can defeat it for free by
issuing the next batch — which is exactly what it already does.

**The premise is not entirely empty.** Gated batches *are* bigger on the arm:

| G1 = actions REQUESTED per stall-gated batch | value |
|---|---:|
| animation_v1 | 2.169 |
| a22_v2_seed1 | 3.000 |
| a22_compaction_v1 | 2.363 |
| **effnote_v1 (the B4=11.11 run)** | **4.490** |

So the direction of B4 reproduces at batch granularity (4.49 vs a control max of
3.00). But the absolute quantity a cap could bite is **~4.5 actions, not 11**,
and the recorded 15- and 16-action gated batches are the level-completing ones.

## 3. THE REDESIGN, AND WHY IT DOES NOT EARN A SLOT EITHER

The brief prescribed the fallback: *"cap only after the batch has already
produced k consecutive no-ops … so a productive batch is never truncated."* We
built the more general form — a **k-tolerant STALE-STATE RUN cap**: abort the
remainder once *k* consecutive executed actions have reached **no board state
new to this level** (a byte-identical no-op is a strict special case). It is
shipped in the module and it is genuinely safe:

| safe rule (LC cut = 0) | saved | saved% | on cleared levels | aborts | mult (= LC-honest) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| `noop_run=1` | 771 | 4.21% | 62 | 45 | **×1.0091** |
| `stale_run=8`, persisted across batches | 541 | 2.96% | 54 | 47 | **×1.0117** |
| `stale_run=6` (the module default) | 429 | 2.34% | 43 | 26 | ×1.0029 |
| `noop_run=2` | 403 | 2.20% | 57 | 26 | ×1.0070 |

Three reasons this does not get slot 2:

1. **The effect is an order of magnitude inside the noise.** ×1.003–×1.012 on a
   ~1.5 local score is +0.005…+0.018. Our per-draw **sd is 0.1513** (ledger
   n = 29). The family `duck-harness-kaggle-continuation-v1` is **m = 2 ⇒ NOT
   SCREENABLE** (SCREEN_PROTOCOL §1 P2 / §4.6). Nothing about this is readable
   from one draw.
2. **The best safe rule is a strict SUBSET of a mechanism that already ran
   live and was scored.** `noop_run=1` on gated batches ⊂ P1 mechanism B
   (`P1_ABORT=1`, `P1_ABORT_NOOP_STREAK=1`, unconditional on the gate), which
   shipped on 08-12, fired 75 batch-aborts, delivered **M0 = 3.68%** and was
   **NO-PROMOTE**. Re-running a weaker version of a measured null is not an
   experiment.
3. **The behavioural endpoint cannot discriminate at this firing rate.** The
   gate arms on **6.2–9.2%** of batches, and the safe rule aborts **26–47 times
   across four whole runs**. P1's live delivery came in at *half* its own replay
   estimate (3.68% vs a replayed 5.9–20.0%). A P2 arm would deliver ~1%, on ~6
   aborts per run. That is not a mechanism reading; it is a foregone null.

## 4. THE BEHAVIOURAL ENDPOINT AND ITS CONTROL SPREAD — computed BEFORE the seal, and left on the record for whoever comes back to this

Sealed per the EFFNOTE discipline (compute the control-side statistic first; a
within-run before/after contrast is not evidence — that is what made P1

> **G1 — actions REQUESTED per stall-gated batch.** The cap truncates
> *execution*; it never shrinks the *request*. So G1 stays a free measure of what
> the agent chose and **cannot be moved mechanically by the cap** — the defect

**Had P2 been pushed, PASS would have required G1 < 2.169** — strictly below the

band: `saved/requested` ∈ [3%, 30%] (P1's M0 band) — **which the safe rule's
replay estimate of 2.34% already fails before any arm exists.**

## 6. WHAT WOULD LICENSE A FUTURE PUSH

* **Not** a bigger *k*, and **not** the position cap at any value. Both are
  refuted above with numbers.
* A mechanism aimed at the object we actually found: **deliberate persistence
  across informed observations**, i.e. the agent re-issuing the next batch after
  seeing the board. That is a *turn*-level phenomenon, not a batch-level one, and
  the only runner-side lever on it that is not already refuted would have to
  constrain the *turn* — with the same lc-safety proof run first, because every
  recorded level completion sits at the end of a long stall-gated burst.
* Any such arm must publish the **LC-honest multiplier**; the naive one is
  systematically inflated by exactly the completions the mechanism destroys.
