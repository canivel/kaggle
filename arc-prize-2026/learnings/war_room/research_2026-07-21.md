## Agentic harness / verification — the on-point new item

**AgentAbstain: Do LLM Agents Know When Not to Act?** ([arXiv:2607.10059](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.10059),
Jul 11) — benchmarks whether agents abstain from acting when they should; best model only **59.5% on paired
tasks** (correct-act AND correct-abstain). Names a failure mode, **"post-hoc abstention"**: the agent executes
an irreversible action *before* recognizing it should have abstained — i.e. it lacks a pre-execution
necessity/verification check and only realizes after the effect.
**Verdict: ADAPT (medium).** This is the abstention-shaped twin of our EWM step-0 abort problem and directly
motivates the **verify-before-act ordering** already in the OPINE/OCM/Rodionov contract we're converging on:
gate each planned action through a pre-execution check against the sim (procedure-validity + phase-augmented
state) rather than discovering misalignment after committing the action. For our runtime the cheap extraction is
a **"should-I-act" pre-flight** on aliased games: if the phase-augmented sim predicts a no-effect action on the
current phase (the exact wa30/sc25 aliasing case from the latent-state audit), abstain/re-observe instead of
burning the turn. Feeds EWM contract v1.1 (resync-before-abort) and the sentinel's turn-conservation goal.

## Agentic harness / budget — reinforces the sentinel line

**When Agents Do Not Stop: Uncovering Infinite Agentic Loops (IAL)** ([arXiv:2607.01641](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.01641),
Jul 2) — defines Infinite Agentic Loops (cost exhaustion, model DoS, context growth, repeated side effects);
ships **IAL-Scan**, static analysis over an "Agentic Loop Dependence Graph" that flags unbounded feedback paths
reaching expensive ops (91.9% precision on 6,500+ repos).
**Verdict: ADAPT (low).** Static-analysis tooling is not directly liftable to our offline runtime, but the
failure taxonomy independently names two of our GSME pathology buckets (**stuck-loop, budget-death**) and the
"unbounded feedback path reaching an expensive op" framing is a clean articulation of exactly what the budget
sentinel (SENTINEL_BUDGET=150) exists to bound. Cite as external support for the sentinel's premise; no build.

**The Harness Effect: Orchestration Design Sets Token Economics** ([arXiv:2607.06906](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.06906),
Jul 8) — a "Writer Agent Harness" cuts blended cost 41% / tokens 38% at quality parity across six foundation
models vs the 2026-06-07 industry-default loop; gains multiply across models regardless of backbone.
**Verdict: PARK (governance).** Enterprise writing tasks, not games, and no mechanism we can transplant. Value:
independent evidence that **harness design is model-agnostic leverage** — a token-efficient 27B harness should
carry to the 72B swap, i.e. the war-v4 A17 screen should port the *current* harness verbatim (which the A17′
draft already mandates via byte-identity reset-path tests) rather than co-designing a new loop for 72B. No action.

## Test-time learning / policy evolution — nothing liftable

**EvoPolicyGym: Autonomous Policy Evolution in Interactive Environments** ([arXiv:2607.02440](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.02440),
Jul 2) — benchmark for agents that iteratively refine **executable policies** under a fixed interaction budget
across 16 compact RL environments; trajectory-level diagnostics on how agents allocate budget and convert
feedback into tuning.
**Verdict: PARK.** Right shape (executable-policy refinement under a fixed budget = our EWM-under-turn-budget
regime) but it's an eval harness on non-ARC toy RL envs with no transferable mechanism. Worth a glance if they
publish the budget-allocation diagnostics — that framing could inform how we credit the sentinel's turn savings.
No build.

**Long-Horizon-Terminal-Bench** ([arXiv:2607.08964](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.08964), Jul ~12) — long-horizon
terminal tasks with dense reward-based grading. **Verdict: IGNORE.** Terminal/coding domain, dense-reward
grading; wrong regime (our runtime is sparse/level-completion, no dense reward). No transfer.

## Serving / war-v4 (72B AWQ, A17 screen) — one concrete NEW data point

No new arXiv throughput anchor for Qwen2.5-VL-72B-AWQ specifically (A17 self-bench remains the reference, as
noted 07-19/07-20). But the 2026 quantization consensus now carries a **concrete quality delta on 70B-class
open weights**: across six models, **FP8 lands within 0.4 pt of FP16** (MMLU-Pro / HumanEval+) while **AWQ-INT4
degrades within ~1.6 pt**; throughput lift batch-1 decode ~1.4× FP8 vs ~3.1× AWQ-4 on H100-class. Reasoning/code
is exactly where INT4's loss is most visible.
**Verdict: ADAPT (sharpens the 07-18 caveat).** This quantifies the risk the 07-18 sweep flagged qualitatively:
the ~1.6 pt AWQ-4 hit is on *text* reasoning benchmarks and does not include VL/perception degradation, so on the
grinder games it is a lower bound on the risk. Concrete A17 action: **if Qwen2.5-VL-72B-AWQ fails the capability
bar (≥8 MAX-levels) on the grinder set, test FP8/W8A8 on the same 96GB rail before declaring war-v4 NO-GO** — the
0.4-vs-1.6 pt gap means FP8 is the correct fallback SKU, and 72B-FP8 still fits 96GB (~72GB weights). Fold into
the A17′ amendment §8.2 "materially different artifact" clause (an FP8 fallback is a bounded, pre-registered SKU
swap, not a materially different artifact).
