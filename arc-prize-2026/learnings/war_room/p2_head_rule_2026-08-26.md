## Why the bar is `CERTIFIED` and not merely "not INFRA DEATH"

`p2_score.py` returns four verdicts, and the two intermediate ones are exactly the
cases where P2 is **the floor plus a cost**:

| verdict | what it means | heads tonight? |
|---|---|---|
| `INFRA DEATH` | certification failed | **no** |
| `CERTIFIED-TRIGGER-UNDERFIRED` | D1 failed: retry armed on <15/25 games | **no** |
| `CERTIFIED-DELIVERED-NOT-USED` | D1 passed, D2 failed: offered and not taken | **no** |
| `CERTIFIED` | armed on ≥15/25 **and** ≥25% of armed turns called `attempt()` | **yes** |

If D2 fails, the agent behaved exactly as the stock agent does — but paid the
prereg's ≈+10% token cost for an affordance it never used. That is the certified
floor **minus** tokens, on a rail where the decision budget is the binding
constraint (675/675 game-runs die on the 7,920 s clock). Spending the nightly draw
on it buys no information we do not already hold from n=5 floor draws, and it is a
strictly worse draw than the filler. A merely-non-fatal verdict does not earn the
head.

**The read is unaffected either way.** D2 failing is a genuine, reportable result
about affordance uptake — it is what killed P1 at 1.3% use against a 30% bar — and
it gets logged as such. This rule governs only which artifact is *submitted*
tonight, not what the arm is judged to have shown.

## What this rule deliberately cannot do

It cannot rescue a late kernel. If v1 is still RUNNING at 18:00 the filler heads,
the P2 artifact is read tomorrow morning, and **nothing about the arm is concluded
from the fact that it missed a clock.**
